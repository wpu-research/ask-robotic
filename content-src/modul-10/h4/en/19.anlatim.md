### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to **play** an instrument with the button and logo inputs
- The difference between sound **with a duration** and **continuous** sound
- How to turn a sensor value into a frequency with the **map** block
- Why you need to turn off a continuous sound with **stop sound**

### What you should know first
Lessons 16-18: playing notes, tempo and variables. You also need to know the **if ... then** block and the card's **light level** sensor (0-255).

### Inputs, sound and a sensor
An instrument has three parts: an **input** (a finger, a button, light), a **rule** (which sound for which input) and an **output** (the speaker). The card has three button combinations: **A**, **B** and **A+B**. You use the three of them for three notes. Touching the logo is a fourth input.

### Sound with a duration and continuous sound
The note blocks so far played for a set time and then went quiet. The **ring tone [N] Hz** block, however, starts the sound and lets go; the sound keeps playing until the **stop sound** block. The two blocks are a pair: one turns it on, the other turns it off. Because a continuous sound counts as having a duration of zero, the "playing" box in the Speaker section may not show it; you still hear the sound.

### Map: turn one range into another
The light sensor gives a number between 0 and 255, but the frequencies that sound nice are between 200 and 1000. The **map** block scales a value from one range to the other:

```
map( light , 0-255 , 200-1000 )
   light   0  -> 200
   light 255  -> 1000
   light 128  -> 602   (near the middle)
```

The calculation: `200 + ışık × 800 / 255` (`ışık` is the light level), then it is rounded to the nearest whole number. The first range is the sensor's, the second range is the sound's. If you mix up the order, the result is nonsense.

### Updating all the time with a timer
Because the light keeps changing, setting the sound once is not enough. The **every [100] ms** hat block repeats what is inside it ten times a second. Inside it you write: **if the logo is touched**, set the sound from the light at that moment. If the logo is not touched, the **if** block skips its inside and the sound does not start. When the logo is released, a separate hat block that calls **stop sound** turns the sound off. If you forget this block, the continuous sound does not stop by itself.

### Summary
An instrument is made of an input, a rule and an output. A note with a duration ends by itself; a continuous sound must be turned off with the stop sound block. The map block scales a sensor range such as 0-255 to a frequency range. The timer hat block keeps the values up to date, and the if block plays the sound only while the logo is being touched.
