### What you will learn in this lesson
- What sound is and how **frequency** (Hz) sets the pitch
- Which frequencies the card's notes match
- That the note block waits until it has finished, which is why notes play in turn
- Why you need to put blocks inside an event hat block (button pressed)

### What you should know first
This week starts with music. From the earlier weeks you only need these: dragging and dropping blocks, the **on button [A] pressed** hat block, the ▶ Run button and showing an icon on the screen.

### Sound is a vibration
When you pluck a guitar string, the string moves back and forth and pushes the air. This back-and-forth movement reaches your ear as sound. The **number of vibrations per second** of the string is called its **frequency**; its unit is **Hz** (hertz). If the frequency is large, the sound is **high**; if it is small, the sound is **low** (deep). The card's speaker does the same thing: you give it a number, and it makes a sound with that many vibrations.

### Notes are numbers
On the card, every note is tied to a fixed frequency. Some of them:

```
Note    Low C      C     D     E     G     A     High C
Hz        131     262   294   330   392   440    523
```

Notice something: **Low C** (131) is half of **C** (262); **High C** (523) is about twice as much. Doubling the frequency raises the sound by one **octave**. It is the same note name, sung higher or lower.

### The note block waits
The **play note [C] for [1/4] beat** block does two jobs: it starts the note and **waits** until its time is up. This waiting is very important. If you put two note blocks one under the other, the second starts after the first:

```
play C  (500 ms)  -> when it ends
play D  (500 ms)  -> when it ends
play E  (500 ms)
Total: 1500 ms
```

The card's tempo starts at 120, and at this speed a 1/4 beat lasts 500 milliseconds. You will look at duration in detail in the next lesson.

### Blocks run inside a hat block
On the card, no block runs by itself. Something has to happen first, such as a button being pressed. This "event" is written with a **hat** block. If the note blocks are not inside a hat block, they look faded in the editor and do not run. To see the code, you can look at the JavaScript tab: `music.playNote("Do", 1)`. This tab is only for reading.

### Summary
Sound is vibration; the number of vibrations per second is the frequency, measured in Hz. A large frequency gives a high sound, a small frequency a low sound. The card's note blocks play at fixed frequencies and wait until they finish, so notes placed one under the other are heard in turn. Notes must be inside a hat block, or they do not run at all.
