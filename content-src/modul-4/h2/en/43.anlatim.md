### Contact switches
A **reed switch** is made of two thin metal reeds inside a glass tube. When a magnet comes near, the reeds touch and the contact **closes**. A **limit switch** (micro switch), on the other hand, is a lever: when a moving part reaches the end point and presses the lever, the contact changes. Both are buttons; what presses them is a magnet or a part instead of a finger.

| Switch | What closes the contact | In the lab |
|---|---|---|
| Reed | A nearby magnet | **Magnet nearby** on the reed part |
| Limit | A part pressing the lever | **Pressed** on the button part |

### Reading with INPUT_PULLUP
One pin of the switch goes to the board pin, the other to GND, and the pin is set to `INPUT_PULLUP`:

```
contact open   -> pin pulled up to 5 V by the internal resistor -> HIGH
contact closed -> pin connected to GND                          -> LOW
```

If you forget the pull-up, the pin is left floating when the contact is open (a floating input) and the reading is random. It is a good idea to flip the reversed logic once in the code: `bool kapali = (digitalRead(REED) == LOW);`.

### NO and NC
Real limit switches have three pins: common (COM), normally open (NO) and normally closed (NC).

| Connection | At rest | When pressed / when the magnet comes |
|---|---|---|
| NO | Contact open | Closes |
| NC | Contact closed | Opens |

In alarm systems the rule is: **make the alarm condition "the contact opens"**. With the contact normally closed (NC connection), when the door opens the contact opens and the pin reads HIGH. Even if the burglar cuts the wire, the contact opens, the pin still reads HIGH and the alarm sounds; the fault does not stay silent. If the alarm condition were "the contact closes" (NO connection), a cut wire would change nothing and the system would be blind. The price is that the logic is reversed in the code: with NC, HIGH = breach, LOW = all is well.

### Bounce and debounce
Mechanical contacts open and close for a short moment as they close. Here is an example timeline (in ms, with a 20 ms stability condition):

```
t=0 LOW, t=3 HIGH, t=6 LOW, t=9 HIGH, t=12 LOW, then stays LOW
```

The timer is reset at every change; since the last change is at t = 12, it is accepted at t = 12 + 20 = 32 ms. Instead of four changes you get one clean event. If there are several switches, each one keeps its own "last change" time; you do this with an array.

### Summary
Reed and limit switches are contacts and are read with `INPUT_PULLUP`: LOW when the contact is closed, HIGH when it is open. Because an NC connection shows even a broken wire as an event, it is preferred in safety applications, but the logic has to be reversed in the code. To filter out contact bounce, a change is accepted only after it has stayed stable for a certain time.
