### Warm objects give off radiation
The human body and every warm object give off invisible **infrared** radiation. A **PIR** (passive infrared) sensor listens for this radiation. "Passive" means it sends nothing itself. Inside the sensor there is a detector that splits the field of view into two halves, and the sensor looks at the **difference in radiation between the two halves**.

A person standing still in a room gives off the same radiation in both halves, so the difference is zero. A person moving from one half to the other creates a difference, and the sensor counts this as "motion". That is why a PIR may not notice someone who is **standing still**.

### A module with three pins
A PIR module has three pins:

| Pin | Job |
|---|---|
| VCC | Supply (the PIR in the lab works on 5 V) |
| OUT | HIGH when there is motion, LOW when there is none |
| GND | Ground |

The module drives the OUT pin itself. That is why on the Arduino you use plain `INPUT`, not `INPUT_PULLUP`. The reversed logic of a button does not apply here: motion = HIGH.

### Warm-up time
When a PIR is switched on, it waits for a while to get used to the heat balance around it. During this time OUT can give random HIGHs and LOWs. On real modules the time is usually from half a minute up to a minute, and it depends on the module. The lab shortens it to 2 seconds.

```
t = 0 s     power on             OUT noisy (ignore it)
t = 2 s     warm-up finished     OUT now reliable
```

In applications such as alarms, the PIR is not read for this long after start-up.

### Hold time and the occupied/empty decision
Even when the motion has ended, a PIR keeps OUT HIGH for a while. In the lab this time is 3 seconds. If you accept this time as the sensor's own decision, you may get "room empty" for someone sitting without moving. The solution: every time you see HIGH, record a timestamp with `millis()`, and only say "empty" once a certain time has passed since that timestamp.

Example: if the last HIGH was seen at t = 23 s and the waiting time is 5 s, the "empty" decision is made at t = 28 s.

```
if (digitalRead(PIR) == HIGH) sonHareket = millis();
bool bos = (millis() - sonHareket >= BOS_SURE);
```

### Summary
A PIR is a passive sensor that detects changes in infrared radiation; its OUT pin goes HIGH when there is motion and is read with plain `INPUT`. After it is switched on, its output cannot be trusted until it has warmed up, so readings are ignored during that time. Instead of treating the output directly as "room occupied", it is more robust to keep the time of the last motion and say "empty" once enough quiet time has passed.
