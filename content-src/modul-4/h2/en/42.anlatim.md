### Send light, listen for the reflection
An IR obstacle module sends out light with an infrared LED and catches the reflected light with a receiver. If there is an object in front of it, more light comes back. Unlike the ultrasonic sensor, it does not measure distance: it decides whether the reflection has passed a certain level or not.

The strength of the reflection depends not only on the distance but also on the object's **colour and surface**. A light-coloured matt surface sends back a lot of light. A dark or shiny surface sends back little and may not be detected even when it is closer.

### Digital and analogue output
The module can have two outputs:

| Output | What it gives | Where the threshold is |
|---|---|---|
| OUT | Obstacle / no obstacle (digital) | On the adjustment screw on the module |
| AO | Reflection strength (analogue) | In the number you write |

On the module in the lab, OUT is LOW when there is an obstacle and HIGH when there is none. On other modules it may be the other way round. That is why it is a good habit to put the level into a constant such as `const int ENGEL_SEVIYESI = LOW;`.

The lab model raises the AO voltage in a straight line with proximity: about 0.25 V at proximity 0 and about 4.75 V at proximity 1. So the `analogRead` value moves between about 51 and 972. At proximity 0.4 you read about 419, and at 0.8 about 788.

### A single threshold flickers
If the AO value is wobbling around 550 and the single threshold is 550, the output keeps switching on and off. The solution is two thresholds:

```
high threshold = 600   (go above this: "obstacle")
low threshold  = 500   (go below this: "no obstacle")
```

In the 500-600 zone in between, the state **does not change**. This is called hysteresis. If the readings come in as 450, 620, 550, 520, 480, 590, there are only two changes: "obstacle" at 620 and "no obstacle" at 480.

### Count the edge
If you are counting boxes on a conveyor belt and you count on every pass while a box stands in front of the sensor, the counter shoots up. The right way is to count once at "the moment it changes from no obstacle to obstacle"; this is an **edge**. On real modules, to filter out the jitter around the edge, you wait for the change to stay stable for a short time (debounce).

### Summary
An IR obstacle sensor looks at reflected infrared light and says "obstacle / no obstacle"; it does not measure distance and it is sensitive to the object's colour. For the digital output the threshold is on the screw on the module; for the analogue output it is set in the code with two thresholds (hysteresis). To count objects, you need to count the change from no obstacle to obstacle, not the level.
