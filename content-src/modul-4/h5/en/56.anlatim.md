### What is inside a joystick?
A joystick is made of **two potentiometers** standing at right angles to each other and **a button** at the end of the stick. The module has five pins: VCC, GND, VRX (horizontal axis), VRY (vertical axis) and SW (button).

```
 5V ──┬── one end of VRX          Stick in middle: wiper halfway -> ~2.5 V -> ~512
      │   wiper ──> VRX (A0)      Stick at end   : wiper at end  -> 0 V or 5 V
 GND ─┴── other end of VRX                                      -> 0 or 1023
```

Each axis is a voltage divider. With the stick in the middle, the wiper sits halfway along the track; the voltage is about 2.5 V and `analogRead` is about 512. Push it fully in one direction and it gets close to 0 V or 5 V, that is, 0 or 1023. In the lab, if the X axis property is 0.25, the voltage is 1.25 V and the reading is about 256.

### Why is there no exact zero?
A button has two states; a joystick has a continuous range. On top of that, the "middle" is not perfect: neither potentiometer stops at exactly halfway. On a real module with the stick released you read a value such as 497 or 530. This is not a fault; it is the nature of the part.

### Measure the centre, add a dead zone
You do two steps:

1. **Measuring the centre.** When the program starts, the stick is assumed to be released; the average of 16 readings is stored as the "centre". The average reduces noise.
2. **Dead zone.** A small range around the centre (for example ±40) counts as zero. A shaky hand or a small drift does not move the robot.

Then the difference is converted with `map` into the range -100...+100. Example: if the centre is 532 and the raw value is 600, the difference is 68; the part outside the dead zone is scaled from the range 40...491 to 0...100, and with integer division the result is 6. That way the value starts from 0 just outside the dead zone instead of suddenly jumping to 40.

### The button
The SW pin connects to GND when the button is pressed. If you set the pin to `INPUT_PULLUP`, a released button reads `HIGH` and a pressed button reads `LOW`. To catch only the moment of the press, you store the previous state and detect the edge; holding the button down does not repeat the event.

### Summary
A joystick is two voltage dividers and a button. With the stick released, the value is about 512 but not exactly 512; that is why the centre is measured at start-up and a dead zone is put around it. The corrected value, in the range -100...+100, is ready to drive a robot or a servo. The button is read with `INPUT_PULLUP`, and `LOW` means pressed.
