### The idea: reverse the tilt
When a gimbal's base tilts, it tries to keep the platform level. In our model the MPU6050 measures the base's tilt, and the servo applies this angle in the opposite direction:

```
egim  = aci - ofset          // the angle taken as "level" with the button is subtracted
servo = 90 - egim            // middle is 90, turn the opposite way
servo = constrain(servo, 10, 170)
```

If the base tilts +20 degrees, the servo goes to 90 − 20 = 70 degrees. If the tilt were 85, you would get 90 − 85 = 5, but `constrain` raises it to 10; the LED lights at the limit.

### Why do we limit it?
If the servo pushes right up to 0 or 180, it strains against its mechanical end, heats up and makes a noise. That is why the range 10...170 is a safe margin. `servo.write(70)` produces a pulse of about 1266 µs (linear between 544 µs and 2400 µs).

### Every job at its own speed
The project has three jobs: catching the button, updating the filter and the servo every 10 ms, and refreshing the LCD and the serial output every 250 ms. `delay()` makes all of them wait: the button is missed and the filter's `dt` goes wrong. Instead, the last run time of each job is stored and the `millis()` difference is checked. The button updates `ofset` only at the moment of the press, using edge detection.

### Open loop
In this system the MPU only measures the base; there is no sensor that checks whether the platform is really level. Control that does not measure the output and correct the error is called **open loop**. It is simple and good enough for a desktop model. In a real gimbal the platform's own angle is measured too, and the difference is corrected all the time (closed loop).

### A note on real hardware
When the servo takes load, the 5 V can drop, and the MPU and LCD may misbehave. In a real build the servo is powered from a separate 5 V supply and the GNDs are joined. The MPU6050 and the LCD work on the same two wires at different addresses (0x68 and 0x27); an RTC does not go on this line.

### Summary
The mini gimbal turns a tilt measurement (MPU6050 and filter) into servo movement. The servo angle is 90 − tilt, limited to 10...170. All the jobs run at different speeds with `millis`. Because the sensor does not measure the output, it is open loop.
