### Why a separate clock?
`millis()` starts counting from zero when the board switches on and forgets everything when the board switches off. You cannot answer the question "what time was it last night?" with it. An **RTC** (Real Time Clock) is a separate chip that counts seconds with its own crystal and knows the calendar. The Arduino just asks it.

### Connecting over I2C
The RTC module uses two data lines: SDA and SCL. On the Uno these are A4 and A5. The module's address is **0x68**. There can be other devices on the same line too, but their addresses must be different. Because the MPU6050 also uses 0x68 by default, we do not connect the two to the same line.

### Reading and setting the time
The library (RTClib) gives the time as a **DateTime** object:

```
DateTime t = rtc.now();
Serial.println(t.hour());      // 0-23
Serial.println(t.minute());    // 0-59
```

There are also `year()`, `month()`, `day()`, `second()` and `dayOfTheWeek()`, which gives the day of the week (0 = Sunday). These functions return numbers and do not add a leading zero: for 5 March, `month()` gives 3, not "03". You add the leading zero yourself.

To set the time, you write `rtc.adjust(DateTime(2026, 3, 14, 23, 59, 55))` (year, month, day, hour, minute, second). The RTC knows the calendar itself: it handles the change of month and year properly. It does not know about summer time or time zones; it only counts the numbers you give it.

`rtc.lostPower()` tells you whether the clock has stopped. If you set the time at every start-up, the time goes back to that moment every time the device restarts. That is why `adjust` is only called if `lostPower()` is true. On a real module, when the main power is cut, a backup battery keeps the clock running. The lab does not model the battery or power cuts; at every ▶ the clock starts from 2026-01-01 00:00:00.

### Alarm = comparison
An alarm is a comparison of the time read from the RTC with the target time. You do the reading every quarter of a second with `millis()`, so that `loop()` can look after other jobs too. To stop the alarm starting again and again during the same minute, you keep "has it gone off today?" in a variable.

### Summary
An RTC is an I2C device that keeps real time even if the plug is pulled. `rtc.now()` reads it and `rtc.adjust()` sets it; `adjust` is not called at every start-up, only when the time has been lost. An alarm is nothing more than comparing the time with a target. Two devices on the same I2C line cannot have the same address.
