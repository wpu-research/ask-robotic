### Three protocols, three ways of thinking
This week you talked to your Arduino in three ways. They all carry data; the difference is in the number of lines, the speed and how a device is chosen.

| | UART | I2C | SPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lines | RX, TX | SDA, SCL | SCK, MOSI, MISO + a CS for each device |
| Clock line | None | Yes | Yes |
| Choosing a device | One to one | Address (7 bits) | CS pin |
| Bits per byte | 10 | 9 | 8 |

### UART: the speed is agreed in advance
In UART there is no shared clock line; both sides must choose the same **baud rate** (bits per second) in advance. A byte takes **10 bits**: 1 start, 8 data, 1 stop. The time of one bit is `1 / baud`. At 9600 baud a bit is about 104 µs, and the byte rate is baud ÷ 10 = 960 bytes/s. If the speeds are different, the other side sees meaningless characters. Also, one end's TX must go to the other end's RX.

### I2C: talking by address
I2C has a clock line, and the speed is chosen with `Wire.setClock()` (100 kHz or 400 kHz). A byte takes 9 clock pulses (8 data + 1 acknowledge). An address byte and one data byte make 18 pulses: 180 µs at 100 kHz, 45 µs at 400 kHz. The RTC (0x68) and the LCD (0x27) are connected together to the same two wires; if two devices have the same address, they clash. For 400 kHz, every device on the line must support it.

### SPI: fast, more lines
In SPI the clock speed is free, and high. On the Uno the highest SCK is half the processor clock: 16 MHz ÷ 2 = 8 MHz. The values that can be set go down by halving: 8, 4, 2, 1 MHz ... So if you ask for 3 MHz, you get 2 MHz: the library picks the highest value that does not go above your request. Each device needs its own CS line, and only one CS may be LOW at a time.

### Which one, and when?
For jobs with many devices that carry little data (RTC, LCD), I2C's two wires are enough. For jobs that carry a lot of data (SD card), SPI's speed is needed. And for talking to the computer, there is UART already.

### Summary
UART has two lines and no clock; the speed is agreed in advance. I2C has two lines, a clock and addresses. SPI is fast but needs a CS for each device. From the number of bits and the speed, you can work out how long a transfer takes.
