### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to combine several sensor and output jobs in one program
- How to split the distance into zones with thresholds and link a behaviour to each zone
- How to turn the distance into a beep interval in proportion with `map()`
- How to run the measurement, the beep and the screen with `millis()` without them waiting for one another

### What you should know first
The measurement and median filter from lessons 37-38; `map()` from lesson 14; the buzzer from lesson 18; `millis()` from lesson 27; the I2C LCD from lessons 32-33.

### Design the system: the table first
Before writing code, put the behaviour into a table:

| Distance | LED | Buzzer | Screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 cm and above | Green | Silent | GUVENLI |
| 50-100 cm | Green | Slow beep | GUVENLI |
| 20-50 cm | Yellow | Beeps getting faster | YAKLASIYOR |
| Less than 20 cm | Red | Continuous sound | DUR! |

Each row in the table turns into a condition in the code. Make the rule at the boundaries clear: which row is "exactly 20" in? In this project `>= 20` is yellow and `< 20` is red.

### From distance to beep interval
Let the beep rate change in direct proportion to the distance: 800 ms at 100 cm, 100 ms at 20 cm. `map(girdi, girdiMin, girdiMaks, çıktıMin, çıktıMaks)` (input, input min, input max, output min, output max) turns one range into another in a straight line:

```
aralik = map(60, 20, 100, 100, 800);   // 60 cm
// (60-20) * (800-100) / (100-20) + 100 = 450 ms
```

`map()` works with whole numbers and throws away the fraction. That is why the distance is turned into a whole number with `(long)`.

### Three jobs, three clocks
The measurement runs every 60 ms, the screen every 250 ms, and the beep on its own timing. Each job has its own `son...` (last...) variable and looks at the `millis()` difference. There is no long `delay()` anywhere; if you used `delay()` for the beep, the measurement and the screen would stop for the length of the beep.

### The measurement goes through the filter
The raw measurement does not make the decision directly. The median of the last 5 measurements is used; a single wrong reading does not produce the wrong colour or a silence. The price is a reaction delay of about 3 measurements, so 180 ms. If there is no echo, the measurement counts as 400 cm: the system stays silent.

### The screen needs small details
An LCD is slow; writing to it every time round upsets the beep rhythm. Pad every line out to exactly 16 characters: if you write "DUR!" after writing "YAKLASIYOR", the old letters stay on the screen. If there is flicker at a boundary (if the measurement goes back and forth between 19.9 and 20.1), the colour keeps changing; the fix is to separate the entry and exit thresholds (hysteresis).

### Summary
The project combines the ideas of a sensor, a filter, thresholds, `map()` and `millis()`. First the behaviour table is written, then each row turns into a condition. The measurement, the beep and the screen run on their own clocks; the median filter weeds out wrong readings and brings a short delay in return.
