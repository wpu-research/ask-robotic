### What does the smart room do?
The smart room uses a PIR (motion), an LDR (light), a DHT22 (temperature), a relay (fan), an I2C LCD and a button together. The logic is simple: if the room is empty, save energy; when someone comes in and it is dark, switch the lamp on; if it is warm, switch the fan on.

### Four states
| State | Lamp | Fan | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISINMA (warming up) | Off | Off | When the warm-up time is up, BOS |
| BOS (empty) | Off | Off | If the PIR sees motion, DOLU |
| DOLU (occupied) | On if dark | On if warm | `BOS_SURESI` after the last motion, BOS |
| KAPALI (off) | Off | Off | Button: between KAPALI and BOS |

The outputs are a direct result of the state. Each pass of the program has three steps: first read the **inputs** (button, PIR, LDR, DHT), then update the **state**, then write the **outputs** (lamp, relay, screen). `delay()` is not used; every job keeps its own time with `millis()`.

### Two hystereses
For the light: when the ADC drops below 300 it is dark, when it rises above 400 it is light; in between, the decision is kept. For the fan: it switches on at 28 °C and off at 26 °C. The two bands stop flashing on and off with noise or small wobbles.

The LDR is between 5 V and A1, and the 10 kΩ resistor between A1 and GND. With the Light property at 0.5, the LDR is about 14.1 kΩ, the A1 voltage is about 2.07 V and the ADC reads about 424; so it is on the "light" side.

### Safe defaults
If the DHT22 returns `isnan`, or the value is outside the sensible range (for example -40...80 °C), the sensor counts as invalid. In that case the fan stays **off** and the LCD shows `DHT HATA`. The PIR is ignored in the ISINMA and KAPALI states.

### From the lab to reality
The lab models the ADC step and timing, but it does not show noise, button bounce or the 5V collapse. In a real room you calibrate the thresholds again for the light in that place, and you power the fan from a separate supply, not from the board. No circuit is ever built with mains voltage (220 V).

### Summary
The smart room is a state machine that separates input, state and output. The light and fan decisions are made with hysteresis. If the sensor is invalid, the fan stays off. A design that works in the lab must be checked separately in a real circuit against noise, bounce and power effects.
