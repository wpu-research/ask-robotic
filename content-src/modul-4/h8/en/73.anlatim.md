### The number depends on the supply voltage
The output (AO) of analogue modules such as soil moisture is in proportion to the supply voltage. In the lab the formula is:

```
AO = VCC x (0.9 - 0.6 x moisture)        moisture: 0 (dry) ... 1 (wet)
```

With VCC = 5 V and moisture = 0, AO is 4.5 V and the ADC reads 921. In the same dry soil with VCC = 3.3 V, AO is 2.97 V and the ADC reads about 608. Same sensor, same soil, a very different number. That is why calibration depends on the measuring condition.

### Two-point calibration
To turn the raw number into a percentage, you measure two known ends: dry and wet. Then you map them to 0 and 100 with `map`:

```
AO = VCC x (0,9 - 0,6 x nem)        nem: 0 (kuru) ... 1 (ıslak)
```

`map` also handles a backwards range (KURU > ISLAK) correctly. Without `constrain`, values outside the ends push the percentage below 0 or above 100. `map` works with whole numbers and throws away fractions.

### A pin is not an ideal supply
In the lab, an Uno output pin comes with an internal resistance of about 25 Ω. If you connect a load to the pin, the 5 V is shared between the internal resistance and the load. For a 220 Ω load, the pin voltage is 5 x 220 / 245 ≈ 4.49 V, and for 47 Ω it is 5 x 47 / 72 ≈ 3.26 V. The current grows too: about 69 mA at 47 Ω, which is above the lab's 40 mA pin limit. That is why loads such as motors or relays are powered from a separate supply, not from a pin.

### What the lab does not show: a collapsing supply
The lab's 5V line is an ideal supply; however much current the motor draws, the voltage does not drop. In reality, when a powerful load starts, the supply collapses and the **brown-out detector** in the microcontroller restarts the board. This does not happen in the lab, so the sentence "the board did not reset while the motor was running" proves nothing for a real circuit.

### A sensor that does not answer
When the supply is missing, the sensor usually does not give an error; it stays silent. On the HC-SR04, this is `pulseIn` returning 0. If you take 0 as "a distance of 0 cm", the alarm goes off wrongly. The right way is to separate out the 0 and leave the output on the **safe side** (off).

### Summary
The number from analogue modules changes along with the supply voltage, so the calibration must be redone when the conditions change. Two-point calibration is done with `map` and `constrain`. The pin's internal resistance lowers the voltage under load. The lab does not show the 5V collapse or brown-out; if a sensor stays silent, you leave the outputs on the safe side in the unknown situation.
