### The sensor gives a number, the decision is yours
A light sensor does not say "dark"; it only gives a number. Your program answers the question "Is this number dark?". To do that, you pick a **threshold** and compare the reading with it.

### Know the direction
In the lesson the LDR is connected between 5V and A1, and the 10 kΩ resistor between A1 and GND. When the light increases, the LDR's resistance drops and the voltage reaching A1 rises. So in this circuit:

```
Light:         0.1   0.2   0.5   0.9
analogRead:     23    53   424   977
```

As the light increases, the value **grows**. If you swap the two parts, the direction flips. Always write the direction of the comparison by looking at the circuit.

### The problem with one threshold
The shortest decision is: `karanlik = (deger < 473)`. If the reading is far from 473, there is no problem. If the reading wobbles around 473 (light, shadow, ADC drift), the result changes on every pass and the LED switches on and off dozens of times a second. Even if the wobble is only between 445 and 488, it crosses the threshold on every swing.

### Hysteresis: two thresholds, one memory
The fix is to use **different** thresholds for switching on and switching off:

```
if (!yanik && deger < ACMA) yanik = true;
else if (yanik && deger > KAPAMA) yanik = false;
```

Here `ACMA = 423`, `KAPAMA = 523`. The range between them (423-523) is the **dead band**: the LED stays as it is. The `yanik` variable is the system's memory; the decision depends on both the reading and the current state. A thermostat uses the same idea (lesson 35).

### How wide should the band be?
- It must be **wider** than the peak-to-peak width of the noise. If the noise swings by 80 units, a 100-unit band is enough.
- It must not be too wide. With a band of 200-800 the lamp comes on only when it is very dark and goes off only when it is very bright: the system becomes **less sensitive**.

You strike a balance between sensitivity and stability. Instead of writing the thresholds as fixed numbers, you can also learn them from the surroundings: measure the smallest and largest value, and put the on threshold at 40% of the range and the off threshold at 60%.

### Summary
A light sensor produces a number; the circuit decides its direction. A single threshold flickers when there is noise near the threshold. Two-threshold hysteresis ends this flicker thanks to the dead band. The band must be wider than the noise, but narrow enough not to spoil the sensitivity.
