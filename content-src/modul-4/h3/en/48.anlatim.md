### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to read the analogue output of soil moisture, rain and water level sensors
- That each sensor's direction (does the value go up or down?) can be different
- How to turn the raw number into a percentage with `map` and `constrain`
- How to calibrate by measuring the dry and wet ends
- That a reading near a threshold flickers, and how to cure it

### What you should know first

### Three sensors, one method
The soil moisture, rain and water level modules all give an **analogue output** (AO). The sensor produces a voltage, and `analogRead` turns it into a number from 0 to 1023. The real job is turning this **raw number** into something meaningful (a percentage, "it is raining", "the tank is full").

### The direction is not the same for every sensor
On the modules used in this lesson, the directions are:

| Sensor | Dry / empty | Wet / full | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soil moisture | raw ≈ 921 | raw ≈ 307 | drops as it gets wetter |
| Rain | raw ≈ 972 | raw ≈ 102 | drops as it gets wetter |
| Water level | raw ≈ 51 | raw ≈ 819 | rises as the water rises |

There is no rule that says "wet means a big number". You learn the numbers and the direction of every new sensor by measuring it dry and wet. Even at 5 V the water level stick does not reach 1023; its highest value is about 819. You need to choose the thresholds by the sensor's real range.

### Turning it into a percentage
In calibration you measure two ends: the dry end and the wet end. `map(ham, KURU, ISLAK, 0, 100)` turns a value between these two into 0-100. `map` also works with ends in reverse order. But `map` also stretches values outside the ends in proportion, so the result is cut with `constrain(yuzde, 0, 100)`. `map` does integer division and throws away the fraction: 66.7 becomes 66.

```
int yuzde = map(ham, 800, 350, 0, 100);
yuzde = constrain(yuzde, 0, 100);
```

### Noise and averaging
An analogue reading can wobble by a few units each time. Taking the average of eight measurements calms the reading. Instead of burying the calibration as fixed numbers, saving it with a command over the serial port (`k` for dry, `i` for wet) is more reliable. If the two ends are very close to each other, `map` gives a meaningless result; in that case no calculation is done.

### Threshold and flicker
If you use a single threshold for rain or level, the state changes quickly when the reading goes back and forth just above and below the threshold. The fix is to use two thresholds (hysteresis), as in lesson 35. The bare metal prongs of a resistive soil sensor rust over time; on the capacitive kind the prongs are insulated, and it usually lasts longer.

### Summary
All three sensors give an analogue number, but their directions and numbers are different. You measure the dry and wet ends, turn the reading into a percentage with `map` and keep it between 0 and 100 with `constrain`. Averaging reduces noise, and hysteresis stops threshold flicker.
