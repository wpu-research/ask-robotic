### A card with no battery
The RC522 module produces a 13.56 MHz radio field. The small antenna in the card collects energy from this field and the chip wakes up. That is why the card has no battery. The chip sends back the **UID** (unique identification number) stored in its memory. On common cards the UID is 4 bytes; for example `AA BB CC DD`.

A UID is not a password: anyone who can read the card learns it, and some special cards copy a UID. A serious lock does not trust the UID alone; this week we will use the card together with a PIN.

### The SPI bus
The RC522 talks to the Arduino over four-wire **SPI**. I2C uses two wires; SPI is faster and needs four wires:

| Wire | Job | Uno pin |
|---|---|---|
| SCK | Clock pulse | D13 |
| MOSI | Data from the Arduino to the module | D11 |
| MISO | Data from the module to the Arduino | D12 |
| SS | Selects the module | D10 (can be changed) |

An RST pin is needed too, and it can go on any pin you like. SCK, MOSI and MISO are fixed by the hardware on the Uno: these three pins cannot be given another job. The real module runs on 3.3 V; connecting it to 5 V can damage it.

### The pin budget
Before you start a project, count the pins. This week the keypad needs 8 pins, the RC522 5 (3 fixed SPI, SS and RST), the servo 1, the buzzer 1 and two LEDs 2; 17 in total. On the Uno, D2-D13 (12 pins) and A0-A3 (4 pins) can be used, and A4 and A5 go to the LCD: 16 pins. They do not all fit at once. In this situation an engineer picks a board with more pins, a pin expander or another solution.

### Serial imitation
While there is no card reader, we imitate a read by typing `KART:AABBCCDD` into the Serial monitor. The program collects the line character by character and checks the `KART:` prefix and the 8 hexadecimal digits. Two hexadecimal digits make one byte: `yüksek * 16 + alçak` (high × 16 + low). The permission decision looks at the UID; where the UID came from (the reader or the serial port) does not change the decision. That is why, when a real reader is added, only the data source changes.

### Summary
RFID reads a battery-free card with a radio field and gets its UID; the UID alone is not safe. The RC522 talks over SPI, and D11, D12, D13 are fixed. The number of pins must be worked out in advance; this project needs 17 pins, and the Uno has 16. Missing hardware is tested with a serial imitation, and the permission logic stays independent of the data source.
