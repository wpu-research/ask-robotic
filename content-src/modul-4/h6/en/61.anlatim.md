### A message made of light
The IR LED at the tip of the remote cannot be seen with the eye, but it can be seen flashing through a phone camera. The message is coded by **how long the light is on and how long it is off**. The Arduino does not read the light; it reads these durations.

### The carrier and the inverted output
The remote's LED flashes 38,000 times a second; this is called the **carrier**. The receiver module only recognises this speed, and filters out slowly changing light such as the sun or a lamp. The module's output works inverted:

```
no carrier  ->  OUT = HIGH (idle)
carrier     ->  OUT = LOW  (pulse)
```

That is why you measure the leading pulse with `pulseIn(pin, LOW)` and the gaps with `pulseIn(pin, HIGH)`.

### The NEC message
The widely used **NEC** protocol goes like this:

| Part | Pulse (LOW) | Gap (HIGH) |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 9 ms | 4.5 ms |
| Bit 0 | 562.5 µs | 562.5 µs |
| Bit 1 | 562.5 µs | 1687.5 µs |

The pulse is always the same; the **length of the gap** tells you whether the bit is 0 or 1. After the leading pulse come 4 bytes: the address, the inverse of the address, the command, the inverse of the command. Each byte is sent least significant bit first. The inverse copy is a check: if the command and its inverse do not complement each other, the message is corrupt and is thrown away. That is why every message has 16 bits that are 1 and 16 bits that are 0, and it takes about 68 ms whichever button is pressed.

### Gathering the bits into a number
You measure the 32 gaps one by one and write them into a number of type `uint32_t`. When a long gap arrives, you set the matching bit to 1:

```
taşıyıcı yok  ->  OUT = HIGH (boşta)
taşıyıcı var  ->  OUT = LOW  (darbe)
```

`1 << i` shifts 1 to the left by `i` places; `|=` adds this bit to the number. Watch out: on the Uno, `int` is 16 bits. If you do not write `(uint32_t)`, everything after bit 16 is lost.

### What is a library for?
`pulseIn` calls make the program wait (they block). In real projects a library such as IRremote is used: it watches pin changes in the background and recognises many protocols. Writing your own decoder, on the other hand, shows you how the job works.

### Summary
An IR remote sends a message in the form of durations by switching a 38 kHz carrier on and off. The receiver's output works inverted: the pulse is LOW, idle is HIGH. In NEC the bits are told apart by the gap; half of the 32 bits are inverse copies and give an error check. Measuring the durations and gathering the bits into a `uint32_t` is the heart of the decoder.
