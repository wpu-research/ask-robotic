### What you will learn in this lesson
- Why two identical motors do not turn at the same speed

- How to write the **proportional (P) correction** rule and choose its sign correctly
- Why the correction does not bring the drift to zero, but only makes it smaller

### What you should know first
- Ticks, tick distance and a separate counter for each wheel (lesson 91)

### Two motors are never the same
You give two motors the same PWM, but one turns a little slower: friction, wear and the voltage drop in the driver are all different. If the right wheel is 10% slower, on a 200-tick run the left makes 205 ticks and the right 185. That is 20 ticks, or about 20.7 cm, of path difference.

### A small difference, a big arc
If the distance between the wheels is L = 12 cm, the path difference turns the robot by:

```
Δθ = (right path − left path) / L = 20.7 / 12 ≈ 1.73 rad ≈ 99°
```

So just a 10% difference turns the robot almost at a right angle over a two-metre run. The reason is that the error **builds up**: once the robot has looked slightly to one side, it travels the rest of the way in that direction.

A practical rule: a 1-tick difference is about 1 cm of path difference, which is about 5° of heading error (1.037 / 12 rad).

### The counter difference tells you the heading error
If the robot is going straight, the two counters should go up equally. The error is defined like this:

```
error = left_ticks − right_ticks       (positive: left ahead)
```

### Proportional (P) correction
The bigger the error, the bigger the correction:

```
pwmSol = BAZ − KP · hata
pwmSag = BAZ + KP · hata
```

If the left is ahead, you turn the left motor down and the right one up. `KP` sets how many PWM of correction to make per tick of difference. Because the PWM is limited to the range 0-255, the result is clipped with `constrain`. For example, if KP = 20 and the error is 3, the left becomes 200 − 60 = 140 and the right 200 + 60 = 260; the right is clipped to 255.

The sign is very important. If you write it the wrong way round, the slow wheel slows down even more and the drift grows.

### A remaining drift that is not zero
The correction only steps in after a difference has appeared; the error is born first, then corrected. That is why the drift gets smaller but does not reach zero. In the simulation, the drift of 137 cm without correction dropped to 6.7 cm with KP = 20, about 20 times less. As KP grows the drift gets smaller, but it cannot be made endlessly large: the PWM is limited and the measurement arrives tick by tick.

### Summary
The same PWM does not turn two motors at the same speed, and this difference builds up and makes the robot draw an arc. The tick difference is a measure of the heading error. P correction speeds up the wheel that is behind and slows down the one in front; its sign must be correct, and the result must be clipped to 0-255. The correction greatly reduces the drift, but because the error appears first, it cannot bring it to zero.
