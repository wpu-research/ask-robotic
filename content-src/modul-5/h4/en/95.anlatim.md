### Plan first: three kinds of movement
A complicated shape is a few simple movements one after another. A single idea covers them all: the left wheel travels `hSol` metres and the right wheel `hSag` metres.

| Movement | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Straight | +d | +d |
| Turn left on the spot | −arc | +arc |
| Left circle | inner path | outer path |

### Turning on the spot
The robot's centre stays in place, and each wheel draws an arc of a circle with a radius of L/2:

```
arc = angle · L/2 = (π/2) · 6 cm ≈ 9.42 cm  →  9.42 / 1.037 ≈ 9.1 ticks
```

Because one tick brings about 5° of uncertainty, fixing the turn as "9 ticks" is dangerous. Instead, you tie the target to the odometry angle: arc = (target − θ) · L/2. The target is **absolute**: 90°, 180°, 270°, 360°. If you are 4° off at one corner, the next corner makes up for it; if you had said "current angle + 90°", the error would build up at every corner.

### A circle
For a circle whose centre line has R = 25 cm, the radius of the inner wheel is R − L/2 and of the outer wheel R + L/2:

```
inner path = 2π · (0.25 − 0.06) = 1.194 m   (about 115 ticks)
outer path = 2π · (0.25 + 0.06) = 1.948 m   (about 188 ticks)
```

The two wheels must finish at the same time; the speed ratio equals the path ratio (0.613). When working out the inner wheel's PWM, do not forget the dead zone: below 51 the motor does not turn. The right formula is `PWM = 51 + (v − 51) · oran`; for PWM 200, 51 + 149 · 0.613 ≈ 142. If you had multiplied directly by 0.613, you would get 123 and the speed ratio would be spoilt.

### Return-to-start error
When the shape is finished, you measure how far from its starting point the robot stopped. In the simulation the error for the square came out at about 2 cm; this is about 1% of the perimeter (2 m). With the P correction switched off (KP = 0), it rose to 10 cm for the same square. Odometry is the robot's own estimate; because of wrong constants the real error may be larger, and the robot cannot see this on its own. That is why, in the end, an outside measurement and a tolerance (for example at most 5 cm, 8°) are needed.

### Summary
A square and a figure of eight are combinations of straight, turn-on-the-spot and circle movements; all of them are written with the idea "the left wheel this much, the right wheel this much". In turns, an absolute angle target stops the error building up. In a circle, the inner and outer wheels travel different distances, and the dead zone is included in the PWM calculation. Success is judged by measuring the return-to-start error from outside and comparing it with a tolerance.
