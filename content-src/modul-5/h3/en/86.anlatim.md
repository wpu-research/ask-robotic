### What does the sensor measure?
A reflective IR sensor (for example the TCRT5000) is made of two parts side by side: an LED that sends infrared light to the floor and a phototransistor that measures the light coming back. The more differently a surface reflects light, the more the reading changes. The module's analogue output turns this difference into a voltage, and the Arduino turns that into a number with `analogRead`.

In the circuit we use in this lesson, the line reads high and the floor reads low:

| Surface | Voltage | `analogRead` |
|---|---|---|
| Line | about 4.2 V | about 860 |
| Floor | about 0.8 V | about 164 |

On some modules the direction is reversed. So look at the raw values first, and write the comparison afterwards.

### Five sensors, one array
The sensors look down at the floor at the front of the robot and are connected from left to right to the pins A0, A1, A2, A3, A4. There are 2.4 cm between them; so the far-left and far-right sensors are 4 × 2.4 = 9.6 cm apart. To read all five with the same code, you put the pins in an array and go through it with `for`. The order is chosen once and stays the same in all the code; if you think A0 is on the right, the robot turns the wrong way.

### Threshold and pattern
We reduce each reading to a single question: "is it greater than the threshold?" If it is, `1` (line); if not, `0` (floor). Because the floor is 164 and the line is 860, 512, halfway between them, is a good start. When the five results are written side by side, a **pattern** comes out:

```
00100   line in the middle
01000   line one sensor to the left
00001   line on the far right
00000   line not visible
```

If a single sensor is on the line, position = sensor number − 2: −2 is far left, 0 is in the middle, +2 is far right. A negative number means "line on the left".

### Blind zone
The line is 2 cm wide. A sensor sees the line if it is at most 1 cm from the line's centre. Because the sensors sit 2.4 cm apart, if the centre is exactly halfway between two neighbours, it is 1.2 cm from both and both see floor: `00000`. The width of this blind zone is 2.4 − 2 = 0.4 cm, which is 4 mm. If the line were 3 cm wide, two sensors would give `1` at once.

### Summary
A reflective IR sensor gives how much light a surface reflects as a voltage. Five sensors are read from left to right in an array, turned into `0` and `1` with a threshold, and the line's position is worked out from the pattern. If the threshold is chosen without any measuring, it goes wrong when the light or the floor changes; and if the sensor spacing is larger than the line width, a small blind zone remains.
