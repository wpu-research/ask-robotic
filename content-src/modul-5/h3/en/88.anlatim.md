### Bang-bang control
In bang-bang control, the output is one of a few fixed options; there are no values in between. The thermostat was either fully on or fully off. In line following there are three options:

| Where is the line? | What does the robot do? | Left wheel | Right wheel |
|---|---|---|---|
| On the left | turns left | slow (0) | fast |
| In the middle | goes straight | fast | fast |
| On the right | turns right | fast | slow (0) |

If the robot is going to turn left, the left wheel slows down or stops; the right wheel keeps turning.

### One sensor or five?
With only the middle sensor, the rule becomes "if I am on the line, turn right; if not, turn left". The robot can never go straight; it keeps zigzagging along the edge of the line. With five sensors, if A0 or A1 sees the line it is on the left, if A3 or A4 sees it, it is on the right; if only A2 sees it, the robot goes straight. The zigzag shrinks a lot.

### Memory of the last direction
What do you do if no sensor sees the line? A variable (`sonYon`) holds which side the line was last seen on: −1 left, 0 middle, +1 right. If a sensor sees the line, it is updated; if not, it stays as it is and the robot keeps turning that way. The decision depends on the current reading and on the past together. Without memory ("if there is no line, go straight"), the robot misses the bend. Measurement on an oval track in the simulation:

| Program | Total time not seeing the line |
|---|---|
| Five sensors with memory | about 3.9 s (lap ≈ 17 s) |
| Five sensors without memory | about 12 s |

### A two-threshold decision
A real sensor's reading flickers near the threshold. With a single threshold, the decision keeps changing as 0-1-0-1. With two thresholds, the decision becomes "on" when the signal first goes above the upper threshold and "off" when it drops below the lower threshold; in between, the old decision is kept. Example: switch on at 600, switch off at 420. Even if the reading wobbles between 500 and 550, the decision does not change.

```
if (!acik && v > 600) acik = true;
else if (acik && v < 420) acik = false;
```

### Summary
Bang-bang control chooses the output from a few fixed options; with one sensor it gives a constant zigzag, with five sensors much less zigzag. The `sonYon` memory makes the robot turn towards the last direction it knew when the line disappears, and cuts the lost time to a third. On a noisy signal, two thresholds (hysteresis) stop the decision from flickering.
