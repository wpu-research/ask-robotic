### No steering wheel, just a speed difference
Robot vacuum cleaners, tabletop racing robots and many research robots steer with two separately motorised wheels. This is called **differential drive**. If the two wheels turn at the same speed, the robot goes straight. If one is faster, the robot curves towards the slower side. If they turn in opposite directions, the robot spins on the spot.

### Describing the robot with two numbers
Let the left wheel's ground speed be vL and the right wheel's vR (m/s, forward positive). The robot's whole motion fits into two numbers:

- **v**: the forward speed of the robot's centre, that is, the average of the two speeds
- **ω** (omega): the turning speed, in rad/s. A left turn is positive, a right turn is negative

```
v = (vR + vL) / 2
ω = (vR − vL) / L
R = v / ω
```

Here **L** is the distance between the points where the two wheels touch the ground (the axle track). On the robot in the lessons, L = 0.12 m and the wheel radius r = 0.033 m. **R** is the radius of the circle the robot draws; if the speeds are equal, R is infinite (driving straight), and trying to calculate the formula is a division by zero.

### Three typical cases
| Case | v | ω | Robot |
|---|---|---|---|
| vR = vL | vR | 0 | goes straight |
| vR = −vL | 0 | 2·vR / L | spins on the spot |
| vL = 0, vR > 0 | vR / 2 | vR / L | turns around the stopped wheel, R = L / 2 |

Example: if vL = 0.10 and vR = 0.30 m/s, then v = 0.20 m/s and ω = 0.20 / 0.12 = 1.67 rad/s. Because the right wheel is faster, the robot turns left.

### The inverse problem
Most of the time you choose v and ω, and the wheel speeds are what you need to find:

```
vR = v + ω · L / 2
vL = v − ω · L / 2
```

The Arduino gives a PWM value, not a speed. Converting speed to PWM is the topic of lesson 78.

### Radians and degrees
ω is in radians per second. To convert to degrees, you multiply by about 57.3. 5 rad/s is about 286°/s; so a full turn takes about 1.26 seconds.

### Summary
In differential drive, the direction changes with the speed difference between the two wheels. v comes from the average, ω from the difference; the number that divides ω is the axle track L. Equal speeds give straight driving, opposite speeds give a spin on the spot, and one speed at zero gives a turn around the stopped wheel. Not mixing up units (metres, radians) is the most important habit in this lesson.
