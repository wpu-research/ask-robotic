### What you will learn in this lesson
- That a motor driver (L298N, TB6612) does two separate jobs: choosing the direction and setting the speed with PWM
- The forward, backward, brake and coast states, using a truth table
- The important differences between the L298N and the TB6612 (voltage drop, STBY, EN jumper)
- The rules for a safe connection: separate motor power and a common GND

### Why a driver board?
An Arduino pin cannot supply the current needed to drive a motor (lesson 22). The motor needs a separate battery and a **motor driver** in between. Inside the driver there is an H-bridge for each motor (lesson 23). The L298N and the TB6612 use the same idea; they can drive two motors independently.

### Two jobs: direction and speed
Each motor has three inputs:

- **IN1, IN2** (AIN1, AIN2 on the TB6612): choose the direction of the current
- **EN** (PWMA on the TB6612): sets the speed; it is connected to a PWM pin

In this module we use EN D5, IN1 D7, IN2 D8 for the left motor and EN D6, IN3 D9, IN4 D10 for the right motor.

### Truth table
| IN1 | IN2 | EN | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| HIGH | LOW | PWM | forward |
| LOW | HIGH | PWM | backward |
| HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | brake |
| LOW | LOW | LOW (0) | coast |

**Braking** connects the motor's two leads together; the spinning motor behaves like a generator and slows down with its own current. When **coasting**, the motor is left disconnected and slows down only through friction. That is why braking usually stops it sooner. The simulation robot in this module does not tell the two apart (in both it slows down with a time constant of about 80 ms); do not mistake this for the behaviour of a real robot.

### L298N and TB6612
- **Voltage drop:** in the L298N, part of the voltage going to the motor is used up in the driver and the board gets hot; the TB6612 (with MOSFETs) is usually much more efficient. For the exact value, check the datasheet.
- **STBY:** on the TB6612, if this pin is LOW the driver is in standby mode and the motor does not turn. Do not forget to make it HIGH.
- **EN jumper:** ready-made L298N boards have a jumper on ENA/ENB that always keeps the motor at full speed. For PWM, remove the jumper and connect EN to a PWM pin on the Arduino.

### Safety and wiring
Do not take motor power from the Arduino's 5 V pin; use a separate battery box and connect the battery's negative to the Arduino GND (**common GND**). Without a common GND the driver cannot understand the signals. Suddenly reversing while going at full speed strains the driver and the battery; stop briefly first. `analogWrite` only works on PWM pins (3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11 on the Uno).

### Summary
A motor driver sets the direction with the IN pins and the speed with the PWM given to EN. The four rows of the truth table are forward, backward, brake and coast. The L298N and the TB6612 work in a similar way, but the TB6612 is more efficient and needs STBY. Separate motor power and a common GND are always needed.
