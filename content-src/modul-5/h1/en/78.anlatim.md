### PWM is not speed
When you write `analogWrite(EN, 128)`, the robot does not go at half speed. The reason is the **dead zone**: before the motor starts turning, it needs enough power to overcome friction and the resistance of the gears. At low PWM this power is not there; the motor hums but the wheel stays still. You see this in real motors too, and it changes with the motor, gears, battery and floor. The dead zone of the robot in the simulation is the first 20 percent of the PWM range: at about 51 and below, the wheel does not turn.

### Above the dead zone it is roughly linear
After the dead zone, the speed rises linearly with PWM and reaches the maximum speed at PWM 255. In the simulation this is 0.396 m/s. So the speed is proportional not to the PWM itself but to the part above the dead zone:

```
v = V_MAX · (PWM − 51) / (255 − 51)
```

### Finding it by measuring
The first PWM you notice by eye (for example 57) is not the end of the dead zone, only the "visible" threshold, because the speed rises slowly from zero. The reliable method is to draw a line through two points that can be measured well. Example: you measured 9.4 cm/s at PWM 100 and 28.8 cm/s at PWM 200.

- Slope = (28.8 − 9.4) / (200 − 100) = 0.194 cm/s per PWM unit
- The PWM where the speed is zero = 100 − 9.4 / 0.194, about 51

On a real robot you can find the speed by timing a 1-metre run with a stopwatch.

### The calibration function
The function that turns the requested speed into PWM skips the dead zone:

```
PWM = 51 + (255 − 51) · v / V_MAX
```

At v = 0 it starts from 51, and at v = V_MAX it becomes 255. Turning very small requests (below 5 mm/s) into zero is also a good habit. The "naive" mapping, which ignores the dead zone completely (PWM = 255 · v / V_MAX), makes a big error at slow speeds: when you ask for 5 cm/s, it gives PWM 32 and the robot does not move at all.

### Calibration is not permanent
As the battery runs down, the maximum speed drops; carpet and wooden floors change the dead zone; another robot's numbers will not fit yours. That is why `OLU_PWM` and `V_MAX` sit at the top of the code as `const` and are measured again when needed. Measure with a nearly full battery and on the floor where you will use the robot.

### Summary
Because of the dead zone, the motor does not turn at low PWM; above it, the speed depends roughly linearly on PWM. The end of the dead zone and the maximum speed are found from two measurement points. The calibration function turns the requested speed into PWM using this line. The numbers belong to a particular robot, floor and battery; when these change, they must be measured again.
