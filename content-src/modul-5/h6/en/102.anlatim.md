### What you will learn in this lesson
- On which time scale the accelerometer and the gyroscope can be trusted
- The complementary filter's formula and the role of the `α` value
- Working out the time constant (`τ`) and estimating the filter's steady-state error
- How the Kalman filter differs from the complementary filter

### What you should know first
From lesson 101: the accelerometer angle (`atan2`), gyroscope speed and bias. From Module 4: a first look at the complementary filter (lesson 59). Decimal multiplication and addition are enough.

### Two sensors, opposite weaknesses
| Sensor | Over a short time | Over a long time |
|---|---|---|
| Accelerometer | Noisy (vibration, jolts) | Correct, because it is tied to gravity |
| Gyroscope (sum of the speed) | Smooth and fast | Drifts, because the bias builds up |

Where one is weak, the other is strong: the two **complement** each other.

### The filter's formula
Every round you first move the angle forward with the gyroscope, then pull the estimate a little towards the accelerometer's angle:

```
aci = ALFA * (aci + hiz * dt) + (1 - ALFA) * ivmeAci;
```

`ALFA` is usually 0.98: 98% of the estimate comes from the gyroscope and 2% from the accelerometer. The two shares must add up to 1; otherwise the filter swells up or dies away. `dt` is the real time between two measurements (in seconds).

### Time constant
The time it takes for the accelerometer's lasting information to show up in the angle is roughly `τ = α · dt / (1 − α)`. Example: for α = 0.98 and dt = 0.01 s, τ = 0.98 · 0.01 / 0.02 = 0.49 s. Vibrations shorter than this are largely filtered out. If `α` grows, the filter gets smoother but corrects the drift late; if it shrinks, the accelerometer's noise passes into the angle.

A small steady-state error remains in the filter. If the gyroscope bias is `b`, the filter's angle error while the robot is still is roughly `b · τ`. With a pure integral, on the other hand, the error grows without stopping. It also matters to start the angle from the accelerometer in the first round; if you start from zero, the filter gives a wrong angle until it settles at the correct value.

### A look at the Kalman filter
The Kalman filter uses the same idea (move forward with the gyroscope, correct with the measurement), but you do not choose the correction share as a constant; the filter works it out at every step by looking at the uncertainty of the estimate and the sensor noise. The cost is matrix operations and extra settings. In this course we carry on with the one-line complementary filter.

### Summary
The complementary filter listens to the gyroscope in the short term and to the accelerometer in the long term. `α` and `dt` set the time constant: τ ≈ α · dt / (1 − α). The bias does not disappear completely but it does not build up; the steady-state error is roughly the bias times τ. The Kalman filter is a heavier relative that works out the share by itself.
