### What you will learn in this lesson
- Why `dt` matters in a control loop, and what a wrong `dt` breaks
- The difference between `delay(5)` and "a loop that runs every 5 ms"
- Writing a fixed-period loop with `micros()` based on a planned time
- Handling overruns (missed rounds) and the `micros()` wrap-around correctly

### What you should know first
From Module 3: non-blocking timing with `millis()` and the `unsigned long` difference (lesson 27). From lesson 102: adding up the angle with `aci += hiz * dt` and the `dt` in the filter. Converting between hertz and milliseconds: 200 Hz = 5 ms.

### Three things that depend on dt
`dt` appears in three places in the control code: the gyroscope integral (`aci += hiz * dt`), the complementary filter's time constant, and PID's derivative and integral terms. If `dt` is wrong, the angle comes out wrong in proportion. Example: while the gyroscope rotates at a constant 50 °/s, if a round really takes 5.35 ms but you assume `dt = 0,005`, in 4 seconds you find about 186.9° instead of the real 200° (6.5% short).

### delay() does not give a fixed period
`delay(5)` means "wait 5 ms". The rest of the round (I2C reading, calculations, `Serial.print`) is added **on top** of it. The round takes `5 ms + the time of the work`, and the work does not take the same time every round.

### A loop based on the planned time
The solution is to keep the **planned** time of the next round:

```
if (micros() - sonPlanUs < PERIYOT_US) return;
sonPlanUs += PERIYOT_US;
```

`+=` stops small delays from building up. If you write `sonPlanUs = micros()`, each delay is added to the next one and the period drifts away from the target. The loop enters the work only when the time is up; in between, `loop()` returns empty; there is no waiting.

### Overrun and wrap-around
If the work takes longer than one round, the round is **missed**. To notice this, you increase a counter when the difference goes beyond two periods, and resynchronise the plan. You still measure the real `dt`, so the filter and the integral are not fed wrongly.

`micros()` counts as a 32-bit `unsigned long` and wraps around after about 71.6 minutes. If the subtraction is done with `unsigned long`, the difference comes out right even across the wrap-around. If you use `int`, it is 16 bits on AVR, so it overflows for differences longer than 32.7 ms.

### How fast?
The period must be much shorter than the robot's falling time scale. In the simulation the uncontrolled robot falls in less than 1 second; at 5 ms and 20 ms the robot is balanced, at 60 ms there is large oscillation, and at 150 ms it falls. Real robots usually choose 5-10 ms.

### Summary
`delay()` makes the round longer by the time of the work. For a fixed period, move the planned time forward with `+=`, count missed rounds, measure the real `dt`, and take differences as `unsigned long`. A slow control loop throws the robot off balance.
