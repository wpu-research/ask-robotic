### Why is "it works well" not enough?
A claim that cannot be measured can neither be proved nor disproved. Before starting, an engineer writes down the **acceptance criterion**: in which measurement, and when I see which number, will I say "done"? The criterion sets both the goal and the exam paper. A criterion written after the robot has been built bends to fit the robot; that is not a criterion, it is an excuse.

### A measurable requirement
A good requirement contains three things: a **number**, a **unit** and a **measurement method**.

| Wish (cannot be measured) | Requirement (can be measured) |
|---|---|
| It should go smoothly | It should finish the course with 0 collisions |
| It should be precise | Tip position error less than 5 mm at all three points |
| It should keep its distance | The target should stay at 20 cm ± 3 cm, that is, between 17-23 cm |

In this week's three projects, the criteria of the paths are written like this: A (autonomous vehicle) finishing without collisions and stopping at the finish line; B (robot arm) an error smaller than 5 mm; C (follower robot) 17-23 cm.

### Limit values and logic
When you turn the criterion into code, two details change the result.

- **Limit:** "less than 5 mm" is a strict statement. If you measure exactly 5.0, you fail; the code uses `<`, not `<=`. "17-23 cm with the limits included", on the other hand, needs `>=` and `<=`.
- **Combining:** in path A, having 0 collisions is not enough; a robot that stands still without moving has not collided either. That is why the criterion is the **and** of two conditions: `carpma == 0 && bitti == 1`. If you use `||`, a robot that collides but finishes also passes.
- **Range:** in path C, you do not look at the average but at the **smallest** and **largest** values seen during the follow. A robot with a good average that came as close as 12 cm for a moment fails.

### Design document
Before the first line of code, one page is filled in: the goal (one sentence), a table of requirements, the subsystems and their interfaces, the state machine, the test plan, the risks. An empty box causes an argument later. The document also records each subsystem's **stand-alone test**; lesson 112 is built on this.

The power rule is a fixed part of the document: motors and servos are powered from an external supply, not from the Arduino's 5 V pin, and the GND is shared.

### Summary
The final robot's test is written before the robot is built: acceptance criteria with a number, a unit and a measurement method. Limit values (`<` and `<=`) and the way conditions are combined (`&&` and `||`) change the result. The design document gathers the goal, the subsystems and the tests on one page. Honesty means not loosening a criterion later, but writing it in the document together with the reason.
