### What is heading, and who measures it?
A roving robot needs to be able to answer the question "where am I facing right now?". This answer is called its **heading**. The robot has two sources:

| Source | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Encoder | Does not drift over time | Moves in coarse steps |
| Gyroscope | Smooth, sees small turns | It measures speed; when added up, bias builds up |

### Heading from the encoders
The difference in the distance travelled by the two wheels turns the robot:

```
heading (rad) = (right distance − left distance) / axle width
```

One tick is 2π · 0.033 / 20 ≈ 1.04 cm of wheel travel. If the axle width is 0.12 m, a single tick moves the heading by about 4.95 degrees. So the heading moves "like a staircase" in steps of 5 degrees. But a counted tick does not build up error: what one tick is today, it is tomorrow too.

### The gyroscope's scale
The gyroscope measures **angular speed** (°/s). To find the angle you add up the speed over time: `açı += gz · dt` (angle += gz · dt). Watch out for two things:

1. **Bias.** Even when the robot is still, `gz` may not be zero. If the bias is 2 °/s, a false angle of 48 degrees builds up in 24 seconds.
2. **Scale.** By default the MPU6050 measures ±250 °/s and gives 131 units for each °/s. If you turn faster, the raw value sticks to the limit; this is called **saturation**. For the ±500 scale, 0x08 is written to the GYRO_CONFIG register (address 0x1B) and the divisor becomes 65.5. If you change the scale and not the divisor, the angles come out wrong.

### Fusing with a complementary filter
The idea is as in lesson 102: trust the gyroscope over a short time and the encoder over a long time.

```
heading = α · (heading + gz · dt) + (1 − α) · encoderHeading
```

If `α = 0,98` and `dt = 0,02` s, the filter's time constant is about 1 second. At every step the heading is pulled a little towards the value shown by the encoder. This way the gyroscope bias cannot build up; the remaining error is about "bias × 1 s". For a 2 °/s bias this is about 2 degrees. If you wrap the heading to 0-360 and then filter it, the gap between 359 and 1 degrees looks like a difference of 358; that is why you run the filter with the unwrapped angle and do the wrapping afterwards if needed.

### Summary
The encoder heading does not drift but is coarse; the gyroscope is smooth but drifts. Choose the gyroscope scale for your robot's fastest turn, and make the divisor match the scale. The complementary filter combines the two: the gyroscope carries the short term, the encoder the long term. This heading is the basis of the position estimate in the next lessons.
