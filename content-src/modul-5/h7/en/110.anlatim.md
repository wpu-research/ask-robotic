### What you will learn in this lesson
- Combining the parts of several lessons in a single robot
- Working out a position estimate (x, y) from the encoder distance and the fused heading
- Wrapping the angle difference into the −180...180 range to head for a target
- Assessing a robot with numbers (coverage, collisions, time)

### What you should know first
- Lesson 94: odometry
- Lesson 106: combining the encoder and the gyroscope (complementary filter)
- Lesson 107: behaviours, suggestions and the arbiter
- Lessons 82 and 84: escaping obstacles with an ultrasonic sensor

### Putting the parts together
This project does not teach a new topic; it links together what you have learned. The robot has three behaviours:

| Priority | Behaviour | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `KAC` (escape) | Front distance below 25 cm (until it goes above 60 cm) |
| 2 | `ZIYARET` (visit) | After the first 12 seconds, if the area has not been visited yet |
| 3 | `DOLAS` (wander) | Always |

For the first 12 seconds the robot only searches. Then a wish to go to an area appears. If an obstacle appears, `KAC` always keeps priority: however strong the wish to reach the target, not bumping into things comes first.

### Position estimate
The robot has no map and no GPS. It works out its position itself: it knows where it started, and every 20 ms it adds the distance it has travelled and the direction it is facing.

```
distance = (left distance + right distance) / 2
x = x + distance · cos(heading)
y = y + distance · sin(heading)
```

The heading comes from the complementary filter in lesson 106. For example, if 0.2 m is travelled while the heading is 60°, x increases by 0.2 · cos60° = 0.10 m and y by 0.2 · sin60° = 0.173 m. The accuracy of this calculation depends on the distance and the heading; the starting values must match the robot's real position.

### Heading for a target: the angle difference
The `ZIYARET` behaviour works out the angle to the target (`atan2`) and looks at its difference from the heading. If the difference is bigger than 30°, it turns on the spot to correct its direction; if it is smaller, it approaches with a speed difference between the two wheels while driving. There is a trap here: if the heading is 10° and the target angle is 350°, plain subtraction gives 340° and the robot turns the long way round. The difference needs to be wrapped into the −180...180 range: 340 − 360 = −20°. Do the wrapping only on the difference; do not wrap the heading variable.

### The position estimate can go wrong
If the robot pushes against a wall and the wheels keep turning, the encoder keeps counting, but the robot does not move forwards. The position estimate drifts away from the real position. Wheel slip has the same effect. That is why you need to measure how far you can trust the estimate, and preventing collisions in the first place (`KAC`) also protects the position.

### Measuring success
Saying "it explored well" is not enough. If you divide the arena into cells and look at how many cells the robot's track passes through, you find the **coverage** ratio: visited cells / total cells. You also measure the number of collisions, the time spent pushed against a wall and the time it reaches the area. Switching a feature off (for example `KAC`) and seeing how the result gets worse is the strongest proof that the feature works.

### Summary
The explorer robot is a combination of behaviours and a position estimate. `KAC` provides safety, `ZIYARET` the task, and `DOLAS` the default behaviour. The position is updated at every step with the encoder distance and the fused heading; the angle difference is wrapped. Success is measured with numbers such as coverage, collisions and time, and with a comparison made by switching a feature off.
