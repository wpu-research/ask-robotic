### The problem: we have a position, we need angles
When you say "go to that point", the arm has a point, but you cannot give a servo a point; you give it an angle. Finding the position from the angles is called **forward kinematics**, and finding the angles from the position is called **inverse kinematics**. Forward kinematics gives a single answer and is easy. Inverse kinematics, however, can have no answer, one answer or more than one.

### Describing the arm
In this lesson there is a flat arm with two parts: the upper arm from the shoulder to the elbow (`L1 = 10 cm`), and the forearm from the elbow to the tip (`L2 = 10 cm`). The shoulder is the origin, x is forwards, y is up.

| Angle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| q1 (shoulder) | The angle the upper arm makes with the horizontal; 0 = forwards horizontal, 90 = straight up |
| q2 (elbow) | How far the forearm closes from the line of the upper arm; 0 = straight, 90 = right angle |

The forearm's direction is `q1 − q2`, so forward kinematics is:

```
x = L1·cos(q1) + L2·cos(q1 − q2)
y = L1·sin(q1) + L2·sin(q1 − q2)
```

### Inverse kinematics: the triangle
The shoulder, the elbow and the target form a triangle. Its sides are `L1`, `L2` and the distance to the target `r = √(x² + y²)`. The elbow angle comes from the cosine rule, and then the shoulder angle is found:

```
cos(q2) = (r² − L1² − L2²) / (2·L1·L2)
q1 = atan2(y, x) + atan2(L2·sin(q2), L1 + L2·cos(q2))
```

Example: target (10, 0). Then r = 10; `cos(q2) = (100 − 200) / 200 = −0,5`, so `q2 = 120°`. All three sides of the triangle are 10 cm (equilateral) and `q1 = 60°` comes out. Check: `10·cos60° + 10·cos(−60°) = 10` and `y = 10·sin60° + 10·sin(−60°) = 0`.

### Unreachable points
- **Too far:** if `cos(q2)` is greater than 1 or less than −1, the triangle cannot be formed. The reach is limited to `L1 + L2`; on this arm, 20 cm. In this case `acos()` gives `NaN`.
- **Servo limit:** if q1 or q2 falls outside 0-180°, the servo cannot go there.

For such a target, write nothing to the servos; let the arm stay where it is.

### Degrees, radians and microseconds
`cos()` and `sin()` want radians; the servo wants degrees. Radians = degrees · π / 180. The servo angle is converted to pulse width with this formula: `544 + açı · (2400 − 544) / 180` µs (with `açı`, the angle). For 90° this is 1472 µs. Watch out for the integer division trap: `(2400 − 544) / 180` comes out as 10 in integers (not 10.31).

### Summary
Forward kinematics finds the position from the angles; inverse kinematics finds the angles from the position. For a two-part arm, the cosine rule is enough. For every target, first check reachability and the servo limits; then write the angles to the servo. Calculating back and comparing with the target is the best way to catch sign mistakes in the formula.
