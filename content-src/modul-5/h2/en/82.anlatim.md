### Measuring distance with sound
The HC-SR04 sends a pulse of sound and waits for the echo to come back. You give `TRIG` a 10 µs pulse; the `ECHO` pin stays HIGH until the echo returns. `pulseIn(ECHO, HIGH, 30000)` measures this time in microseconds. Going there and back, sound spends about 58 µs for each 1 cm:

```
distance (cm) = time (µs) / 58
```

Example: if the echo took 2900 µs, the obstacle is 2900 / 58 = 50 cm away. Because the time is there and back, the number 58 is twice the 29 µs for one way.

### The measurement jumps: the median
A sensor looking at the same wall again and again does not always give the same number. In the simulation there is a wobble of about ±3 cm, and it happens on a real sensor too. Now and then a completely wrong measurement may arrive. An average is spoilt by such an outlier; the **median** is not. For the median, sort the measurements and take the middle one:

| Measurements | Sorted | Median | Average |
|---|---|---|---|
| 44, 41, 90, 45, 43 | 41, 43, 44, 45, 90 | 44 | 52.6 |

90 is an outlier. The median ignores it and stays at 44; the average, however, shifts to 52.6. In the simulation measurement, the median wobbled about half as much as a single measurement.

### Choosing a threshold: sensor and braking margin
The robot stops when the distance drops below the threshold; `ESIK_CM = 25` means "stop when the sensor is 25 cm from the obstacle". Do not forget two things:
- The sensor is about 6 cm in front of the robot's centre. At the threshold, the robot's centre is about 25 + 6 = 31 cm from the obstacle.
- The robot does not stop the moment you say `surus(0, 0)`. Because of motor lag, it moves about 1.5 cm further.

If you choose a small threshold, the risk of a crash grows; if you choose a large one, the robot cannot get through narrow gaps.

### If no echo comes back
If the sensor sees an obstacle that is very close, very far away or that scatters the sound, it may not receive an echo. `pulseIn` returns 0 when it times out. If you read this 0 as "0 cm, I am right at the wall", the robot stops even in an open space. In the lesson, if there is no echo we return `400`: "very far".

### Summary
The HC-SR04 measures time, and you find centimetres by dividing by 58. Because a single measurement jumps around, take the median of several measurements before making a decision. When choosing the stop threshold, take into account that the sensor is at the front of the robot and that the robot also travels some distance while stopping. Handle the no-echo case separately.
