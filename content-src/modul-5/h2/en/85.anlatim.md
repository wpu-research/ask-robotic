### "It works" is a number
"The robot is doing well" is not a measure. Two numbers are a good enough start:

| Measure | What does it tell you? | Is it misleading on its own? |
|---|---|---|
| Distance (m) | Is the robot really moving forward? | Yes: it can also travel a long way by crashing into walls |
| Number of collisions | Is it safe? | Yes: a robot that never moves never collides either |

Read the two together. If there are few collisions and the distance is small too, the robot is standing still most of the time.

### Count collisions correctly
In the simulation, while the robot is touching a wall, the flag (`collided`) stays true the whole time. If you count it on every pass, a single contact looks like hundreds of collisions. The solution is the edge detection from lesson 8: count the moment the flag changes **from false to true**.

### Derive the threshold from measurements
In the project the criterion is: at least 3 m travelled and at most 3 collisions in 30-35 seconds. The threshold was chosen like this: the robot goes at 0.2 m/s; if it never stopped, it would do 7 m in 35 seconds. But each obstacle round (stop, scan, turn) interrupts forward motion for about 2.2 seconds. Example calculation: 5 rounds come to 11 seconds, which leaves 24 seconds, and at most 24 × 0.2 = 4.8 m can be travelled. The lowest result measured was 4.0 m; the threshold was chosen as 3 m. If the criterion were 7 m, nothing could pass; if it were 1 m, it would measure nothing.

### Extra protection: stuck check and emergency stop
- **Stuck check:** while the robot drives forward, the distance should change. If it has changed by less than 6 cm for 1.5 seconds, the robot has got caught somewhere and backs off. At a speed of 0.2 m/s, a 6 cm change takes about 0.3 seconds; 1.5 seconds is well above this.
- **Emergency stop:** when `x` arrives from the serial port, the motors stop; when `g` arrives, it starts again. When starting, put the state machine back to a clean start (`gec(ILERI)`); otherwise the robot carries on from the unfinished movement.

### Summary
Track a robot's success with two measurable numbers, such as distance and number of collisions. Count collisions with edge detection, and derive the threshold from measurements. The stuck check and the emergency stop are layers of protection in the code against cases the sensor cannot see. Question the threshold: "does it only fit our arena, or is the robot really good?"
