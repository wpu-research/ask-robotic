### A bumper is a button
Two small switches are placed at the robot's front corners. When the bumper touches an obstacle, the switch closes. This is no different from the button in lesson 6: one end is connected to the pin, the other end to GND, and the pin is set to `INPUT_PULLUP`.

| Bumper state | What the pin reads | The `bool` in the program |
|---|---|---|
| Free | HIGH | `false` |
| Pressed (touched an obstacle) | LOW | `true` |

The reading is written like this: `bool sol = (digitalRead(2) == LOW);`. Here `== LOW` is the question "is it pressed?". If you turn the answer to this question into a `bool` variable once, the rest of the code reads with the word "pressed".

### Which bumper, which direction
Picture the robot from above:

```
      front
  [LEFT]--[RIGHT]
     |      |
     +robot-+
```

| Left | Right | Meaning | Turn the program chooses |
|---|---|---|---|
| pressed | free | Obstacle on the left | Right |
| free | pressed | Obstacle on the right | Left |
| pressed | pressed | Straight ahead | Right (because left is pressed) |

The rule is simple: turn away from the obstacle. The code in the lesson only asks `if (sol)`; in a head-on bump the left bumper is pressed too, so it turns right.

### Three steps: stop, back off, turn
1. **Stop:** `surus(0, 0)`. Because of motor lag (80 ms), the robot still slides 1-2 cm.
2. **Back off:** the robot in the lesson goes backward for 400 ms. In the simulation this covers about 5.5 cm. That is less than the 0.2 m/s × 0.4 s = 8 cm you would calculate, because the motors do not change speed instantly.
3. **Turn:** a 450 ms turn on the spot comes to about 90°.

If you skip the back-off, the robot turns while stuck to the obstacle and rubs against it. Do not change the order.

### The bumper's limit
A bumper only notices an obstacle when it touches it. In the simulation, when the bumper is pressed the robot's centre is about 10 cm from the wall. Because the robot's radius is 7.5 cm, there is a 2.5 cm margin left; sliding uses up part of this margin. On a real robot, bumpers bounce (lesson 8), only protect the front corners and may not be pressed by low or soft obstacles. That is why a sensor that warns before a bump (lesson 82) is needed.

### Summary
A bumper is a button on the robot's nose, and with `INPUT_PULLUP` it reads LOW when pressed. Which bumper was pressed tells you the direction of the obstacle; the robot turns away from it. The reaction to a bump goes in the order stop, back off, turn. Because a bumper only reports after a bump, it is good as backup protection but not enough on its own.
