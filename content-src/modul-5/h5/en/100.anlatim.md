### Same skeleton, different error
The project has two robot tasks: line following and wall following. Both follow the same skeleton: **measure, find the error, apply PID, give it to the motors**. The only difference is the source of the error: on the line it is the array position (unit: sensor spacing), at the wall it is the ultrasonic distance (cm). Because the units are different, you need to measure Kp, Ki and Kd separately for the two tasks.

```
u = Kp x e + Ki x integral + Kd x turev
left wheel  = TABAN - u
right wheel = TABAN + u
```

### Is PID better than bang-bang?
We tested the claim with numbers. At full speed, on two tracks, PID was compared with the best bang-bang setting.

| Track | PID lap | Bang-bang lap | Deviation from the line (RMS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard (tight bend, steep slalom) | 8.62 s | 9.04 s | PID 10.9 mm, bang-bang 27.5 mm |
| Easy | 9.86 s | 8.90 s | PID 11.1 mm, bang-bang 12.5 mm |

The result is not a clear "PID is better". On the hard track PID was both faster and closer to the line. On the easy track, however, bang-bang finished about 10% faster. Bang-bang is sensitive to tuning: of the turn values tried, only one finished the track.

### I and D on the line array
The array gives the error in steps of 0.5, and the line may briefly disappear while it passes between two sensors. These jumps create fake bursts in the derivative and fake build-up in the integral. When Ki = 1 was switched on, the lap on the hard track got longer, from 8.62 s to 9.40 s. That is why on the line we used `Ki = 0` and a very small, filtered Kd: the real work is done by P.

### I and D in wall following
At the wall the measurement is continuous and the motors are not equal. With Kp = 16, Ki = 1, Kd = 4, the robot stayed within ±2 cm of the target after the 4th second and did not hit the wall. P alone also held the band, but PID made the error RMS smaller by about a third. If the motor difference is large (15%), P alone cannot hold it and I makes a difference.

### An honest comparison
To be fair, the track, TABAN and the measurement method must be the same, and at least two runs must be done. Which one is "better" depends on what you look at: lap time, closeness to the line or how calm the steering is.

### Summary
The line and the wall fit the same PID skeleton; only the error and the gains are different. On the line array P is enough; I and D are sensitive to fake jumps. In wall following, D removes the overshoot and I the steady-state offset. PID is not always faster than bang-bang; the decision is made with repeated measurements under the same conditions.
