### Seeing the speed too
A driver who is approaching the edge fast corrects the steering straight away, even while still far from it. They do not just look at "how far away am I", but also at "how fast am I getting closer". Its counterpart in control is the **derivative** (D): the rate of change of the error. In the code it is called `turev` (derivative), and the previous error is `sonHata` (last error).

```
turev = (e - sonHata) / dt
```

Example: if the error was 5 cm in the previous round, is 4 cm now and the time between rounds is 0.05 s, the derivative is (4 - 5) / 0.05 = -20 cm/s. A negative derivative tells you the error is shrinking, so the robot is getting closer to the target.

### PD control
```
u = Kp x e + Kd x turev
```

When the error starts to grow, D sees it at once and brakes in the opposite direction. While the error is shrinking, D acts gently. This effect is called **damping**. With P alone, a growing Kp throws the robot beyond the target; in the wall-following simulation, when Kp went from 16 up to 48, the overshoot rose from 3.8 cm to 7.5 cm and the robot touched the wall. At the same Kp values, adding Kd made the overshoot several times smaller.

### The error in wall following
The servo turns the ultrasonic sensor to the left. The error is `e = mesafe - HEDEF`, and `e > 0` means the robot is far from the wall. In this case the robot needs to turn towards the wall, that is, to the left. The direction rule is as in lesson 96: `u > 0` turn left.

### Noise and the filter
If the measurement jitters, the derivative amplifies that jitter. Even a 1 cm wobble produces a fake speed of about 20 cm/s over a 50 ms interval. D multiplies this fake speed by Kd and sends it to the motor, and the motors start to jitter. The cure is to filter the derivative (`turevSuz` is the filtered derivative):

```
turevSuz = ALFA * turevSuz + (1 - ALFA) * turev
```

ALFA = 0 means no filter. When ALFA = 0.8, only 20% of the new derivative is taken. In return, D reacts a little later.

### Choosing Kd
First, with Kd = 0, increase Kp until you see overshoot. Then try Kd at roughly a tenth of Kp, and increase it if the overshoot gets smaller. If the motor starts to jitter, decrease Kd or make the filter stronger. The numbers change from robot to robot; the rule is to measure.

### Summary
The D term looks at the rate of change of the error and damps the overshoot: `u = Kp x e + Kd x turev`. With P alone, a large Kp throws the robot beyond the target; Kd prevents this. With a noisy measurement, unfiltered D produces fake speeds and makes the motor jitter. That is why the derivative is filtered with ALFA and Kd is kept small.
