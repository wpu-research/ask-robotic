### The steady-state error of P control
A car with a slightly flat right tyre pulls to one side when you let go of the steering wheel. On a robot, this is a weak motor. To correct this pull, P control has to give a constant steering. But because `u = Kp x e`, a non-zero u can only be produced by a non-zero error. The result is a **steady-state error**.

In numbers: if a constant steering of 13 PWM is needed to balance things and Kp = 6, the error has to stay at `13 / 6 = 2,2 cm`. If you increase Kp, the error gets smaller but does not close completely.

### Integral
The **integral** adds up the error over time:

```
integral += e * dt
u = Kp x e + Ki x integral + Kd x turev
```

Even a small error makes the integral grow if it lasts long, and this increases the steering. When the error drops to zero, the integral keeps its built-up value, so the constant correction carries on. In the simulation, with the right motor 15% weaker, the offset stayed at 2.25 cm with `Ki = 0` and dropped to 0.09 cm with `Ki = 2`.

As Ki grows, the offset closes faster, but after a point overshoot begins. Start small.

### Windup
The motor cannot get more than 255 PWM. If the robot is given a speed target it cannot reach (target 50 cm/s, the motor's limit about 39.6 cm/s), the error does not close and the integral keeps growing. This swelling is called **windup**. When the target later drops, the swollen integral first has to drain; during this time the output stays high and the robot cannot slow down.

Measurement: after 4 seconds of saturation the integral rose to 40.2, and the robot took 1.84 s to settle at the new speed.

### Anti-windup
There are two common methods:
1. **Limiting the integral:** `integral = constrain(integral, -ILIM, ILIM)`. At most `Ki x ILIM` reaches the output.
2. **Stopping when saturated (conditional integration):** while the output is stuck at 0 or 255, `integral += e * dt` is not done.

With the second method, in the same run the integral stayed at 1.8 and the recovery took 0.34 s. Normal operation before saturation was not spoiled. If you choose ILIM too small, you cannot close the steady-state error: `2 x 3 = 6 PWM` is not enough for the 13 PWM needed.

### Summary
P control leaves a steady-state error; the integral closes this error by accumulating it. When the output saturates, the integral swells up; this windup delays the move to a new target for a long time. Limiting the integral or stopping it when saturated prevents windup. Ki is started small, because a large Ki leads to overshoot.
