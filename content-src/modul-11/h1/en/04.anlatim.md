### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to give a sprite that moves on its own a **speed** (x speed and y speed)
- Which way minus and plus speed values go
- What the **bounce** setting does
- How to give a direction in degrees (0 up, 90 right)
- How to tile the background with a picture

### What you should know first
Lesson 1: x and y coordinates, and that y grows downwards. Lesson 2: that speed is "pixels per second" and distance = speed x time. Lesson 3: that a block can run only once and its effect lasts.

### Speed is two numbers
In lesson 2 we linked speed to the keys. Now we give the sprite its own speed. Speed has two parts: the **x speed** tells you the sideways movement, the **y speed** the up-and-down movement. Both are in pixels per second.

| Speed | Direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| x speed plus | right | x grows to the right |
| x speed minus | left | x gets smaller |
| y speed plus | down | y grows downwards |
| y speed minus | up | y gets smaller |

A minus speed means going the opposite way. You run the speed block once; the value stays stored on the sprite, and the sprite keeps going at the same speed until another block changes it. The `stops` block sets the speed to zero.

A calculation: if a ball with an x speed of 40 starts at x 20, after 3 seconds it is at the point x 20 + 40 x 3 = 140.

### Bounce
The **bounce** setting decides what happens when a sprite reaches the edge of the stage. With `bounce off walls/edges: yes` on, when the sprite hits an edge, the speed in that direction turns the other way: plus becomes minus, minus becomes plus. So the ball stays inside and keeps going back and forth. With the setting off, the sprite goes out through the edge.

Watch out: the stay on screen setting from lesson 2 stops the sprite at the edge; bounce sends it back. The two do different jobs.

### Direction in degrees
Instead of giving x and y, you can also give a direction and a speed: `top moves in direction 90 degrees at speed 40`. The degrees are read like a compass:

```
        0 (up)
270 (left)   90 (right)
       180 (down)
```

45 degrees is exactly halfway between up and right; so it is the top right diagonal. Watch out: to go up you write 0; 90 takes it to the right.

### Tiled background
The `tile background with picture Grass` block lays a small (16 x 16 pixel) picture side by side across the whole stage. Instead of a single colour, you get a floor such as grass, sand or water. This block must be under `when game starts` too.

### Summary
Speed is two numbers, the x speed and the y speed; its unit is pixels per second. A minus number takes it the opposite way. Once speed is given, the sprite keeps going until it stops or its speed changes. The bounce setting reverses the speed of a sprite that hits an edge. If you want to give a direction, 0 is up, 90 right, 180 down, 270 left.
