### What you will learn in this lesson
- The difference between the world and the screen
- How the camera follows a sprite and where it stops
- Why tiles need to be turned into real sprites
- How to put the camera and the turn-into block in the right place

### What you should know first
Lesson 11 (map, tile, 16 pixels) and lesson 13 (deleting tiles from the map, score). Remember the collision event between sprites from week 2 too.

### The world is big, the screen is small
The screen is fixed: 160 x 120 pixels. The map, though, can be bigger than this. The plain in this lesson is 30 tiles wide:

```
30 tiles x 16 pixels = 480 pixels
480 / 160 = 3 times the width of the screen
```

The screen is a window onto this world. The **camera** decides where in the world the window is.

### The camera follows the cat
The `camera follows sprite [kedi]` block tries to keep the cat in the middle of the screen. The camera can scroll from the left edge of the world to the right edge; it does not go past the limit. The camera's x value is at least 0 and at most the world width minus 160.

```
cat x = 200 pixels  -> camera x = 200 - 80 = 120
cat x = 30 pixels   -> the camera stops at the left edge, x = 0
cat x = 470 pixels  -> the camera stops at the right edge, x = 480 - 160 = 320
```

That is why no empty space shows at the edge of the world. To let go of the camera you use `camera stays still`; the camera stays where it is.

### A tile is a picture, a sprite is a player
The apple tile on the map is only a picture; it cannot take part in the collision event needed for collecting. The `turn all [Apple] tiles into [Food] sprites` block clears all the apple tiles on the map and puts a **Food** sprite in the place of each one. Sprites can move, take part in collision events and be deleted.

### Order and name
- In the turn-into block, **Food** must be chosen as the kind; the collecting code waits for this kind.
- Always give the camera the `kedi` variable. Outside an event, `this sprite` can be the sprite made last, and the camera may follow the wrong sprite.

### Summary
The world is the map, and the screen is a 160 x 120 window onto the world. The camera keeps the sprite it follows in the middle, but it stops at the edge of the world. When the tiles on the map are turned into sprites, they can be collected or made to move.
