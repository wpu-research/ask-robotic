### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to combine several small rules in one game
- How to plan a game by breaking it into pieces
- How a single rule is applied to enemies of the same kind
- How to think about winning and losing conditions together

### What you should know first
Lesson 16 (patrol), lesson 17 (following and distance), lesson 19 (shooting and collision), lesson 9 (the lives rule). The random number from lesson 18 is also used to choose where the ghosts appear.

### Plan first
A big game is not written in one go. First we write what we need in a table:

```
Part       What it does                     Which event
Bat        goes back and forth at the top   when game starts
Ghost      comes from the top, chases       every 900 ms
Laser      goes up with A                   when A is pressed
Hit        gives points, checks for a win   Bullet hits Enemy
Lives      a life is lost if an enemy hits  Player hits Enemy
```

Each row is a separate event, and they run independently of each other. If you break one, the others keep working; this is very useful when you are looking for a bug.

### One rule, two enemies
The bat and the ghost are both of the **Enemy** kind. That is why the `Bullet hits Enemy` rule applies to both of them at once. When you add a new enemy, you do not write a new rule; you just choose Enemy as its kind. This is the real power of using kinds.

### Making ghosts
The `every 900 ms` event creates a new ghost sprite, puts it at a random x at the top of the screen (y = -8) and makes it follow the hunter. With a 900 ms interval, about one ghost appears every second. The ghost's speed is 15 and the hunter's speed is 80: the player has time to escape and to shoot. If a ghost is 112 pixels away from the hunter directly below it, it takes 112 / 15, about 7.5 seconds, for the centres to meet; because the sprites have a size, they touch earlier, at about 6.4 seconds.

### Winning and losing
Each hit raises the score by 1. At the end of the hit rule, an `if score >= 5 do` block shows the winning message and ends the game. You put this check **inside** the hit event, because that is the only place where the score changes. For losing there is the lives rule: when the hunter touches an enemy, lives go down by 1; if it touches 3 times, the lives run out.

### Summary
A big game is the sum of small rules. Make a table first, and build each part in its own event. Enemies that share a kind use the same rule. A timer makes enemies, collision events change the score and lives, and the win check goes where the score changes.
