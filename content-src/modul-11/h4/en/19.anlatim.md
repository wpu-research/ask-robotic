### What you will learn in this lesson
- How a new sprite (a bullet) is created while the game is running
- Why the sprite kind (Bullet, Enemy) matters in a collision rule
- Who the `this sprite` and `other sprite` blocks are
- Why you must delete bullets you no longer use

### What you should know first
Lessons 16-18: speed and direction. From earlier weeks: making a sprite and giving it a kind, creating a variable, the `button is pressed` event, the collision event and the score.

### A bullet is a sprite too
The laser is nothing special: it is an ordinary small sprite that moves fast. The difference is that it is created **later**, when the player presses a key. It is not on the screen when the game starts. When the `when A button is pressed` event runs, a `new sprite` block runs and adds a new laser to the stage.

You say where to put the laser. To make it come out of the ship's nose, you make its x the same as the ship's and move its y 12 pixels up:

```
lazer x = gemi x
lazer y = gemi y - 12
lazer speed = x 0, y -140
```

A minus y is up. A speed of -140 means the laser goes 140 pixels up every second. If the speed stays at 0, the laser hangs where it is.

### Every press, a new bullet
The event runs once, at the moment the button is pressed. Every press means a brand-new laser sprite. Holding the button down does not fire again and again, because holding it does not count as a new press. If you press 4 times a second, you get 4 new lasers a second. If a laser stays on the screen for 1.5 seconds, you see at most 4 x 1.5 = 6 lasers at the same time.

### Kind and collision
We write the collision rule as `when a Bullet sprite hits a Enemy sprite`. The rule looks at the sprite's **kind**, not its name. If you create the laser with the `Object` kind, the rule never recognises it and nothing happens. Inside the event there are two special blocks: `this sprite` is the kind written first in the rule (here the laser), and `other sprite` is the kind written second (here the alien).

### Tidying up
The collision event runs only once, at the moment the two sprites overlap. If you do not delete the laser, it carries on its way even after the alien explodes. You clear away the laser that hit with `delete sprite this sprite`, and the target with `make other sprite explode`. For lasers that fly off the top of the screen without hitting anything, you also need the `delete when it leaves the world: yes` setting; otherwise the stage slowly fills up with bullets.

### Summary
A laser is an ordinary sprite created when a button is pressed. Its position is set to the ship's nose and its speed to a negative y. The collision rule looks at the kind; `this sprite` is the first kind and `other sprite` the second. Delete the bullet that hits, and clear away the bullet that misses with `delete when it leaves the world`.
