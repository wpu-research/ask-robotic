### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to judge, with questions, whether to leave a decision automatic
- The patterns human in the loop, safe default, hard limit, logging and continuous measurement
- How to get the expected number of mistakes from the error rate in an eval
- Where responsibility stays

### What you should know first
It is enough to know evals and how to calculate accuracy (lessons 16-20), hallucination (lesson 36) and the rule/model split in robots (lesson 38).

### Four questions
Before leaving a decision automatic, ask:
1. **Can it be undone?** A wrong suggestion in a music playlist is easily fixed; a wrong braking decision is not.
2. **Does it affect others?** If a person's safety, money or opportunities are involved, oversight should increase.
3. **How costly is a mistake?** Is the error rate in the eval acceptable?
4. **Can it be explained?** Can you say why the decision was made?

### When the error rate turns into a real number
A system makes 500 decisions a day, and its error rate in the eval came out at 2%. Expected mistakes: 500 x 0.02 = 10 decisions. The percentage looks small, but 10 wrong decisions a day can be too many for a job where the harm is large. Always turn the rate into a daily number and think about it.

### Design patterns
- **Human in the loop:** the model makes a suggestion, a human approves. Example: the model prepares a message, you press send.
- **Safe default:** if it is not sure, stop or ask a human.
- **Hard limits:** limits such as speed, force, area and budget are applied independently of the model.
- **Logging and audit trail:** what was done, and why, is written down; it can be reviewed later.
- **Continuous measurement:** when the system or the data changes, the eval runs again.

A safe default can be seen in a small piece of code:

```
guven = 0.55
esik = 0.80
if guven >= esik:
    print("otomatik uygula")
else:
    print("insana sor")
```

Because the confidence (`guven`) stays below the threshold (`esik`), the output is `insana sor` ("ask a human"); otherwise it would be `otomatik uygula` ("apply automatically"). The threshold is also a design decision; it is tuned with an eval.

### Responsibility
The person who uses the model and the team that builds the system are responsible for the outcome. "The model said so" is not an excuse. Before using it, ask: "Have I checked this myself? Can I live with the consequences?"

### Summary
Whether it can be undone, who is affected, the cost of a mistake and explainability decide the level of automation. Human in the loop, safe default, hard limit, logging and continuous measurement are common patterns. Turn the error rate into a daily number. Responsibility always stays with people.
