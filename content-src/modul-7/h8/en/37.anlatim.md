### What you will learn in this lesson
- Which information you should not type into an AI service
- What anonymising personal data means
- How to present work prepared with the help of AI honestly
- What bias is, and how to measure it with an eval

### What you should know first
It is enough to know that an eval collects cases and works out accuracy (lessons 16-20), and that a hallucination is output with no source (lesson 36).

### Privacy: where does what you type go?
The text you type into a service can be stored or used according to that service's rules. These rules change from service to service and are written in the privacy policy. So get into two habits: read first, then share. Never type these: passwords and keys, addresses and phone numbers, private photos, other people's personal information.

### Anonymising
If your project needs real people's data, remove the names. This process is called **anonymising**. A simple example:

```
Before:  "Ali Veli is 14 years old and lives in Ankara."
After:   "Student A is 14 years old."
```

Changing the name may not be enough on its own: when details such as age, school and neighbourhood come together, the person can still be recognised. The safest thing is not to write details you do not need at all.

### Copyright and honesty
The rights to training data and generated content differ from country to country, and the rules are still developing. When you are not sure, ask your school or an adult. Your part is clear:
- If you used AI, **say so** and follow the homework rules.
- Check quotes and sources yourself; a model can make up sources.
- Presenting work you had a model do as your own work is a problem of honesty.

### Bias
**Bias** is when a model systematically gives worse or stereotyped results for some groups. The cause is often the data: if a group was represented rarely or in stereotyped ways in the data, the model learns that. To measure it, build two groups of similar cases and compare their accuracy:

```
Group A: 40 cases, 36 correct  ->  90%
Group B: 20 cases, 12 correct  ->  60%
Difference: 30 points
```

The overall accuracy (48 / 60 = 80%) looks good on average, but it hides the problem in group B. That is why you need to look group by group instead of at the total.

### Environment and cost
Running large models uses energy. Not sending needlessly long and repeated requests is a good habit.

### Summary
Do not type passwords, addresses, phone numbers or other people's information into a service; anonymise when you need to. Say when you had help from AI, and check the sources. To measure bias, build groups of similar cases, work out the accuracy of each separately and look at the difference.
