# Privacy, copyright and bias
duration: 25

## Wonder
Where does what you type into an AI assistant go? Who owns a generated image or text? Is the model equally fair to everyone? These are ethical questions as much as technical ones.

## Activity
This is a reading lesson; there is no activity tool.

### Privacy
- How the text you type into an AI service is stored and used depends on the service's privacy policy. Not every service is the same: do not share before you read it.
- **Do not share:** passwords, identity details, addresses, phone numbers, private photos, other people's personal information.
- If you need to use real people's data in homework or a project, anonymise it first (remove the names).
- Do not write keys and passwords into code, or paste them into a model.

### Copyright and sources
- The rules about the data used to train models and the rights to generated content vary from country to country, and are still developing. When you are not sure, ask your school or an adult.
- When you use generated content, **say that you used AI** and follow the homework rules. Having a model do your homework and presenting it as your own work is a problem of honesty.
- Check quotes and sources yourself.

### Bias
A model learns the patterns in the data it was trained on. If some groups are represented rarely or in stereotyped ways in the data, the model can carry these patterns into its output. To test this, you use an eval: compare similar cases for different groups. As you did in week 4, you cannot measure bias without writing cases.

### Environment and cost
Running large models uses energy. Not making needlessly long and repeated requests is a good habit.

### Test yourself
1. Which three pieces of information should you never type into a model?
2. How do you measure bias?
3. How would you present homework you prepared with the help of AI to your teacher?

## Discuss
If you wrote three rules for using AI at school, what would they be? Compare them with your friends'.
