### What you will learn in this lesson
- What the **context window** is and what it contains
- What happens when the window is full, and how to work out a token budget
- Where to put important information in a long text
- How to fit a lot of data (like sensor readings) into a model

### What you should know first
From lessons 2 and 3, that a token is not a word but a piece of text; from lesson 6, that the model produces probabilities at every step by looking at the input.

### Window = the number of tokens the model can see at that moment
A model can process only a limited number of tokens in a single request. This limit is called the **context window**. All of these go inside the limit:
- The system message (lesson 8) and the request you write
- The earlier conversation
- The documents or tool outputs you add
- **The answer itself** that the model will write

The last item is often forgotten. If the window is 1000 tokens and your input takes 900 tokens, at most 100 tokens are left for the answer. We can call this the **token budget**: budget = window - input.

### What happens when the window is full?
Real systems handle overflow in different ways: they may give an error, drop old messages or summarise them. The simulation in the Workshop uses the simplest way: the **newest** tokens stay, and the **oldest** tokens are left outside.

The example in the Workshop: the sentence `Robot duvarı görür ve sağa döner. Sonra ileri gider ve engeli görür, durur.` (The robot sees the wall and turns right. Then it goes forward, sees the obstacle and stops.) becomes 36 tokens with 40 merges. If you set the window to 12, 24 tokens are left outside; only the part `leri gider ve engeli görür, durur.` stays in the window. The start of the sentence, `Robot duvarı görür ve sağa döner. Sonra`, is wiped from the model's view. Because tokens are pieces of words, the cut can also fall in the middle of a word: the leading `i` piece of the word `ileri` (forward) is left outside, and the `leri` piece stays in the window.

### Not everything inside the window is used equally well
Even if the information fits in the window, the model may not always use it well. In very long contexts, it has been reported that information in the middle is missed more easily than information at the start and the end. So:
- Write the most important rule short and clear, and put it at the end or the start if needed.
- Take out the unnecessary parts; a short context is often better.
- In a long chat, remind the model of the important rule now and then.

### Choosing and summarising data that does not fit
A robot can produce hundreds of sensor lines every second; they do not all fit in a window. As an engineer, you choose the data: send only meaningful changes, take the last N measurements, or replace the old part with a short summary. Summarising loses information, so you must first decide what cannot be left out.

### Summary
The context window is the total number of tokens a model can see in one request; the input, the history, the documents and the answer all go into it. If the window fills up, part of the text is cut off or summarised; in the Workshop the oldest tokens are left outside. Keeping important information short and clear and removing unnecessary data is the safest habit.
