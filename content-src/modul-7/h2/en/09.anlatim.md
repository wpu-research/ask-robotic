### What you will learn in this lesson
- The six parts of a good prompt: task, role, context, example, format, constraint
- How to turn a vague request into a clear one step by step
- How the checker in the Workshop works out its score
- The limits of a rule-based checker

### What you should know first
From lesson 8, the concepts of role and system message; from lesson 7, that long and unnecessary text fills the window.

### The model cannot read your mind
When you tell a person "sort this out", they may understand what you mean thanks to a shared past. The model sees only the text you write. It guesses what you did not write, and the guess is not always what you wanted. That is why a prompt is a task description written with no gaps.

### Six parts
| Part | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Task | What is to be done? | "Summarise the announcement." |
| Role | Who should the model act like? | "You are an experienced robotics mentor." |
| Context | For whom, and for what purpose? | "For 13-year-old students." |
| Example | What does the answer you want look like? | "Input: ... Output: ..." |
| Format | What should the answer look like? | "At most 3 bullet points." |
| Constraint | What should it not do? | "Do not use technical terms." |

You do not need all of them in every request; but if a part is missing, the model fills it in with its own guess.

### Improving it step by step
Start: **"Summarise the announcement."** There is a task, but nothing else.
1. Add the audience and the purpose: "Summarise the announcement for 13-year-old students, so they don't miss the meeting."
2. Add the format: "Write at most 3 bullet points."
3. Add a role and a constraint: "You are an experienced robotics club mentor. ... Do not use technical terms."

Each step turns something the model would have to guess into something you say.

### How does the checker in the Workshop score?
The checker is not an AI; it is a small **rule-based** program. It looks at keywords and format in the text. The weights of the parts are:
```
task 20 · role 15 · context 15 · example 20 · format 20 · constraint 10   (total 100)
```
"Summarise the announcement." covers only the task part: 20 points. Short prompts (fewer than 6 words) get at most 30 points. If the role and the example are missing and the others are there, the score is 100 - 15 - 20 = 65; the Workshop request "Summarise the announcement for 13-year-old students, so they don't miss the meeting. Write at most 3 bullet points." also gets 65 in this way.

### The limit of the checker
A rule-based checker does not know meaning. If you write "bullet bullet bullet", it ticks the "format" box. It also cannot spot a request like "summarise this", where what is meant is unclear. A high score means "the parts on a checklist are there"; it does not say the request is really good. The real test is to try the prompt on a model and measure the output (week 4). Padding it out is not good either: every sentence should do a job.

### Summary
A good prompt clearly states the task, role, context, example, format and constraint parts, because the model guesses what is not written. The checker in the Workshop counts and scores these parts using rules, but it does not know meaning. The real measure is to try the prompt and look at the output.
