### What you will learn in this lesson
- The rule "measure first, then move", and how it is applied to a robot script
- The principle of least privilege: giving an agent only the tools it needs
- How to tell which steps need human approval
- That the surroundings can change between measuring and moving

### What you should know first
Lessons 26-28: tool calls, carrying results with `$n`, `ileri`, `dur`, crashes and the step limit. Subtraction and percentages are enough.

### Information first, then action
Gathering information before an irreversible action is the basis of working safely, for people and agents alike. It is like feeling your way before walking through a dark room. For the virtual robot, this means measuring with `mesafe()` before calling `ileri(...)`.

### Measure, calculate, move
In the corridor the wall is 100 cm away. Goal: stop 20 cm from the wall.

```
mesafe()           # observation: 100
hesapla($1-20)     # observation: 80
ileri($2)          # observation: 20 (remaining distance)
dur()
```

Here, 20 cm is a **safety margin**. If you make the margin 5 cm, `ileri(95)` leaves the robot 5 cm from the wall; it does not crash, but the tolerance is very small. The smaller the margin, the more dangerous a small measurement error is.

### Measuring and moving are separate steps
`mesafe()` only tells you the situation at that moment. The surroundings can change after the measurement (someone may have put an obstacle in the corridor). That is why real robots keep measuring while they move. If you skip the measurement and write `ileri(90)` straight away, you do not crash in the workshop, because you already know the wall is at 100 cm. But you put that information into the script by hand; if the wall is somewhere else, the script cannot notice.

### Permissions and approval
Every tool you give an agent is a permission:

- **Least privilege:** for a job that only says "report the distance", `mesafe()` is enough; giving it `ileri` is unnecessary.
- **Read first, then write:** gather information before changing anything.
- **Human approval:** for irreversible or risky steps (deleting a file, sending an email, starting a heavy machine), a human approves.

These three rules reduce the number of tools and limit the damage a mistake can do.

### Summary
A safe agent measures before acting, does the calculation with a tool and leaves a safety margin. A measurement is a one-off snapshot; because the surroundings can change, observation continues while moving. An agent is given only the tools it needs, and human approval is asked for irreversible steps.
