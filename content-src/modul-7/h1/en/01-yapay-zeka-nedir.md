# Artificial intelligence, machine learning, language models
duration: 20

## Wonder
You type "my robot keeps hitting the wall" to an AI assistant and you get a sensible answer. But does the thing giving that answer understand you, or is it just very good at predicting? In this module we will look for the answer not by gut feeling, but with small tools you write yourself.

## Activity
This is a reading lesson; there is no activity tool. First, get to know how the module works: the tools run in your browser and the calculations are real. Any step that stands in for a language model is always labelled "simulation" or "template". We do not run real models like Claude on this portal; we build the ideas they rest on in scaled-down form.

### Three concepts
- **Artificial intelligence:** the broad name for systems that do jobs people say need intelligence. A list of rules can count too.
- **Machine learning:** methods that learn from examples instead of having their rules written by hand. You give examples, and the system finds the pattern itself.
- **Language model:** a model that learns from text. Its basic task is to look at what has been written so far and predict the next piece (token). Large language models like Claude are this idea at a very large scale.

### Why do we say "predict"?
During training, a language model learns from a huge amount of text which pieces follow which. When it answers, it works out the probabilities of the next piece, chooses one, then works them out again. As the scale grows, this simple loop leads to surprising abilities. But the same loop can also produce wrong sentences that look confident. You will see this in detail in lesson 36 (hallucination).
