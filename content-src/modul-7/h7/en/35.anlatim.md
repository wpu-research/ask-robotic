### What you will learn in this lesson
- What the red team and blue team roles do
- How to report a finding as finding, impact, recommendation and remaining risk
- How to spend a defence budget thoughtfully
- The ethical line between finding a security weakness and misusing it

### What you should know first
Lesson 33 (attack levels, blacklists) and lesson 34 (the six defence layers, costs). Also the habit "prove it, do not just believe it" from lesson 32: every sentence in your report should be backed by something you saw in the workshop.

### Two teams, one goal
The **red team** tries to break a system; the **blue team** protects it. Their shared goal is to make the system safer. A weakness the red team finds turns into a to-do list for the blue team. Real teams do this with written permission and within a clearly limited scope.

### The two halves of the project
```
Red:   Write a document of at least 5 words that gets past level 0 and level 1.
Blue:  Build a set that stops all six attacks with a total cost of at most 4.
```
Because the cost limit is 4, there are options. For example, "least privilege" (3) and "instruction hierarchy" (1) make 4 in total and close all six attacks. Alternatively, hierarchy, output filter and human approval make 3 in total, leaving one unit of budget spare. Which one you choose depends on what the system is most afraid of losing; justify it in the report.

### What the report contains
| Section | Question |
|---|---|
| Finding | Which text got past which level? Why is level 1 not good enough? |
| Impact | If the attack were real, what would be lost? |
| Recommendation | Which layers, and why? |
| Remaining risk | What does your chosen set not guarantee? |
| Responsible disclosure | If you find a real weakness, what do you do? |

The remaining risk section is the most valuable. The simulation stops all six attacks, but in real models the picture is less certain; saying "completely safe" would be wrong.

### The ethical line
Finding a weakness, telling its owner and helping to fix it is engineering. Testing without permission, misusing a weakness or looking at other people's data can be illegal and does harm. Testing is done only on systems where it is allowed; you report what you find to the owner first, responsibly.

### Summary
The red team takes on the attack and the blue team the defence; together they make the system stronger. A good report honestly writes down the finding, the impact, the recommendation and the remaining risk. The budget limit forces you to decide which layer is worth more. In security, permission and responsible disclosure are essential.
