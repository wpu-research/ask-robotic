### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to set up all the steps of a RAG pipeline together
- How the chunk size, overlap and K settings affect each other
- How to measure retrieval accuracy and the answer's faithfulness to the source separately
- What RAG solves and what it does not

### What you should know first

### The settings depend on each other
If you make the chunk size smaller, the number of chunks grows and each chunk is more focused; but the answer can be split. Overlap softens this. If you increase K, your chance of finding the answer goes up, but the context gets longer and unrelated text gets in. A good setting is not one magic number but a balance you have tested.

### Same document, different questions
The same pipeline works well on some questions and badly on others. In the club guide in the workshop, the question `Bronz rozet için ne gerekir?` ("What do you need for the bronze badge?") carries telling words such as `bronz` (bronze) and `rozet` (badge), and brings back the right sentence. The question `Rozetler hakkında bilgi` ("Information about badges"), on the other hand, is vague; the pipeline may push another sentence to the top. Questions fall into three groups: those whose answer is in one chunk, those whose answer is spread over two chunks, and those whose answer is not in the document at all.

### Two separate measurements
In a RAG pipeline, two things are tested separately:
- **Retrieval accuracy:** is the right chunk in the top K? If the right chunk was retrieved for 14 of 20 test questions, the accuracy is 14 / 20 = 70%.
- **Faithfulness:** is the answer based only on the context, or did the model add something that is not in the context?

This split makes debugging easier. If retrieval is wrong, you fix the chunk size, the question or K. If retrieval is right but the answer is wrong, you look at the prompt template and the model.

### What does RAG not solve?
RAG increases the information in the model's hands; but it does not guarantee that the source is correct. If the guide contains wrong or out-of-date information, the pipeline brings it back as the right chunk and the answer comes out wrong. For questions whose answer is not in the document, the system should say "I don't know"; you should test this separately in the eval set.

### Summary
The settings of a RAG pipeline are a balance that changes together. Retrieval accuracy and faithfulness are measured separately. In the workshop the first four steps are real calculations, and synthesis is a template. RAG does not fix the source: if the document is wrong, the answer is wrong too.
