### What you will learn in this lesson
- How the retrieval step works (rank by similarity, take the best K)
- How the chosen chunks are inserted into a prompt template
- How a vague question spoils retrieval
- Why the instruction "If it is not in the context, say you do not know" is needed

### What you should know first
Lessons 21-22: vectors, cosine and the limit of count vectors. Lesson 23: the five steps of RAG, chunks and overlap. Lesson 9: writing a clear prompt.

### Retrieval: rank and take K
The retrieval step does three jobs: it turns the question into a vector, works out the cosine similarity with every chunk, then ranks by similarity and picks the best **K** chunks (top-K). In the workshop a chunk with a similarity of 0 is not picked. So even if you set K = 5, if a question word appears in only one chunk, only one chunk gets into the context.

```
similarities: p1=0.52  p2=0.31  p3=0  p4=0  p5=0.18
K = 4 -> first four in order: p1, p2, p5, (one at 0)
the 0 is dropped -> 3 chunks get into the context
```

### Question quality
Retrieval only looks at the words the question and the chunk share. `Yardım` ("Help") is a single word; every chunk that contains it becomes a candidate, and the right chunk may not be picked. `Mentörler ne kadar sürede yanıt verir?` ("How quickly do the mentors reply?"), on the other hand, carries telling terms such as `mentör` (mentor) and `yanıt` (reply). A clear question means good retrieval.

### Insertion: the prompt template
The chosen chunks are numbered and put into a template:

```
Answer the question based on the context below.
If it is not in the context, say "I don't know".

Context:
[1] ...
[2] ...

Question: ...
```

The "say I don't know" instruction in the template is important. Without it, the model may also make up information that is not in the context. You can only test whether the instruction works with questions whose answer is not in the context.

### Synthesis is a simulation
In the workshop, step 5 is not a real model. The template quotes the best-matching sentence in the context, and the box says so clearly. A real model's answer is based on interpretation and can also be wrong. That is why you need to look for retrieval mistakes and synthesis mistakes separately.

### Summary
Retrieval ranks the chunks by similarity and puts the best K into the context. A vague question produces poor retrieval. The context is inserted into a prompt template, and the template should contain an "I don't know" instruction. The final answer in the workshop is a quote; do not forget that it is not a real model.
