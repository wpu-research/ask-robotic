### What you will learn in this lesson
- The five steps of RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)
- Why a long document is split into chunks
- What happens when the chunk size is too large or too small
- How to work out the number of chunks with overlap by hand

### What you should know first
Lesson 21: vectors and cosine similarity. Lesson 22: the limit of count vectors. Lesson 7: a model's context window is limited.

### Why do we chunk?
If the information you want to ask about is not in the model's training, the model may guess. The fix is to put the document into the prompt. But a 200-page document may not fit in the window, and even if it fits, it is expensive. RAG does this:

1. **Chunk:** split the document into small chunks.
2. **Vectorize:** turn each chunk and the question into a vector.
3. **Retrieve:** pick the K chunks closest to the question.
4. **Insert:** put the chosen chunks into the prompt.
5. **Synthesis:** the model writes an answer based on the context.

In the workshop the first four steps are real calculations. The fifth step is a template, and the interface labels it that way.

### Chunk size is a balance
In a very large chunk, text unrelated to the question also gets into the context and the similarity is "watered down": the answer is one sentence, the chunk is a hundred words. In a very small chunk the answer can be split across two chunks, or mean nothing on its own. There is no single right size; it depends on the document and the questions.

### Overlap
**Overlap** means consecutive chunks share a few words. If the answer falls on a chunk boundary, overlap makes sure it is complete in at least one chunk. The cost: the same words sit in more than one chunk, and the number of chunks grows.

### Working out the number of chunks
If the chunk size is B and the overlap is O, each new chunk moves forward B − O words. Example: a 100-word document, B = 20, O = 5.

```
step = 20 - 5 = 15
chunk starts: 0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90
last chunk: 90-100 (10 words)  ->  7 chunks in total
```

The last chunk may be short; once the document ends, no new chunk is started. The overlap must be smaller than the chunk size; otherwise the window does not move forward.

### Summary
RAG gives the model the right context by splitting the document into chunks and bringing back the ones that fit the question. If the chunk size is large, the context is watered down; if it is small, the answer is split. Overlap protects answers on the boundaries but increases the number of chunks. You work out the number of chunks from the step (size − overlap).
