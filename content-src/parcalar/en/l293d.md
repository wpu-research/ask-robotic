# L293D motor driver
slug: l293d
category: Power
summary: A chip with two H-bridges inside. You set a motor's direction with IN1 and IN2, and its speed with EN.

## Pins
- VCC1: The chip's logic power (5 V).
- VCC2: The motor power.
- GND: Ground (the real chip has four GND legs; in the lab it is a single pin).
- EN12: Turns the first channel on/off; speed with PWM.
- IN1: First channel direction input 1.
- IN2: First channel direction input 2.
- OUT1: First motor lead.
- OUT2: Second motor lead.
- EN34: Turns the second channel on/off.
- IN3: Second channel direction input 1.
- IN4: Second channel direction input 2.
- OUT3: Second channel's first motor lead.
- OUT4: Second channel's second motor lead.

## How it works
The L293D uses the **H-bridge** idea to change the direction of the voltage across the motor's leads. If IN1 and IN2 are different, the motor turns: IN1 HIGH and IN2 LOW means forward; IN1 LOW and IN2 HIGH means reverse. If they are the same, the motor stops. If EN12 is LOW, the outputs are left floating; if you give EN12 a PWM signal, the speed changes.

The chip has built-in kickback diodes, so you do not need separate flyback diodes. It supplies at most 600 mA per channel.

**Advanced:** The output transistors lose about 1.4 V on the high side. A high output is VCC2 − 1.4 V, and a low output is about 0.2 V. If VCC2 = 5 V, the motor sees about 3.3 V.

## Wiring in the lab
| From | To |
|---|---|
| L293D **EN12** | Uno **D6** |
| L293D **IN1** | Uno **D7** |
| L293D **IN2** | Uno **D5** |
| L293D **VCC1** | Uno **5V** |
| L293D **VCC2** | Uno **5V** |
| L293D **GND** | Uno **GND** |
| L293D **OUT1** | Motor **M1** |
| L293D **OUT2** | Motor **M2** |

If VCC1 or VCC2 is not powered, the lab shows a warning. In a real circuit, if the motor needs more than 5 V, VCC2 is powered from a separate source and the GNDs are connected together.

## Typical values
- Channel current | 600 mA | Real chip limit
- Voltage loss | ≈ 1.4 V | On a high output
- Low output | ≈ 0.2 V | Above GND
- VCC1 | 5 V | Logic power
- Motor (VCC2 = 5 V) | ≈ 3.3 V | Lab model

## Common mistakes
- **Not connecting VCC1 or VCC2.** The chip does not work or the output stays floating.
- **Forgetting GND.** The chip does not work.
- **Not driving EN12, or keeping it LOW.** The motor stops.
- **Setting IN1 and IN2 the same.** The motor stops.
- **Connecting the motor supply to VCC1.** A voltage above 5 V can damage the logic side.
- **Connecting a big motor.** The chip overheats.
