### Changing direction
In the transistor circuit the motor always turns the same way. To make a DC motor turn the other way, you only need to swap its plus and minus terminals. Instead of doing this by hand, you use a circuit made of four switches whose shape looks like the letter "H": an **H-bridge**. The switches close in diagonal pairs; which diagonal closes decides which way the current flows through the motor.

### L293D
The L293D contains two H-bridges and kickback diodes; that is why you do not need a separate flyback diode. It gives at most 600 mA per channel.

```
Pin    Job
EN12   Switches the channel on/off, speed with PWM
IN1    Direction input 1
IN2    Direction input 2
OUT1   Motor terminal 1
OUT2   Motor terminal 2
VCC1   The chip's logic power (5 V)
VCC2   Motor power
GND    Ground
```

### Direction table
```
IN1   IN2   Motor
HIGH  LOW   Forwards
LOW   HIGH  Backwards
LOW   LOW   Stops
HIGH  HIGH  Stops (like a brake)
```
The direction is set by IN1 and IN2 being **different**; the speed is set with PWM on the EN pin. For example, `analogWrite(EN, 200)` gives a duty cycle of 200 / 255 ≈ 78%. When EN is low, or the chip's supply is missing, the outputs float and the motor stops.

### Lost voltage
The L293D loses about 1.4 V at its output. If VCC2 = 5 V, a high output is about 3.6 V and a low output is about 0.2 V; the motor sees about 3.3 V. The same motor turns more slowly than in the transistor circuit.

### Changing direction safely
Reversing a turning motor suddenly strains the motor and its supply. First `dur()`, a short wait, then the new direction. The `ileri()`, `geri()` and `dur()` functions make the code readable.

### Summary
The L293D is an H-bridge chip that sets the motor's direction with IN1 and IN2 and its speed with EN. VCC1 is for logic power, VCC2 is for motor power; GND is a must. The chip drops about 1.4 V, so the motor turns a little more slowly. Stop the motor before changing direction.
