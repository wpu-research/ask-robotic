### What you will learn in this lesson
- Why an Arduino pin cannot drive a motor directly
- That a **transistor** is a switch that controls a large current with a small current
- How to work out the base resistor value and the base current
- What a **flyback diode** does and how it is connected
- How to set the motor speed with `analogWrite`

### What you should know first
Ohm's law from lesson 2, the potentiometer and `analogRead` from lesson 11, PWM and `analogWrite` from lesson 16.

### Why is a pin not enough?
An Arduino pin works comfortably with about 20 mA; going above 40 mA damages the pin. A small DC motor, however, draws hundreds of mA while it runs. The solution: the pin does not control the motor, it controls a **switch** that drives the motor. The power reaches the motor from the 5V line; the pin only says "on" or "off".

### NPN transistor: a switch that works with current
A transistor has three legs: **B** (base, the control), **C** (collector, where the load current goes in) and **E** (emitter, the GND side). When the base-emitter voltage goes above about 0.7 V, a small base current flows and the transistor lets a much larger current through between collector and emitter. The gain in the lab is about 100: 1 mA of base current allows at most 100 mA.

The base resistor limits the current. With D6 = 5 V and 1 kΩ, the base current is about (5 − 0.7) / 1000 = 4.3 mA; this is enough for a load current of up to 100 × 4.3 = 430 mA. If you forget the base resistor, the current is unlimited and a warning appears.

### MOSFET: a switch that works with voltage
A **logic-level MOSFET** conducts when its gate (G) gets a voltage above about 2 V, and it draws almost no current. Not every MOSFET switches fully on with 5 V; you choose a "logic level" one.

### Flyback diode
A motor is a coil. When the current is cut suddenly, the coil makes a high voltage spike in the reverse direction and can damage the transistor. A diode connected in parallel with the motor, in the reverse direction, gives this current a path. The striped end of the diode (the cathode) faces the motor's plus side, that is 5V.

```
5V ──┬── motor ──┬── C (transistor)
     │           │
     └── K ◄ A ──┘      diode: A to the collector, K to 5V
```

### Speed with PWM
If D6 switches on and off quickly, the motor sees the average voltage. `analogWrite(6, 128)` gives a duty cycle of about 50%. The motor in the lab turns at full speed at 6 V; the speed is about voltage / 6.

### Summary
The pin controls the transistor with a small current; the motor's power comes along a separate path. An NPN needs a base resistor, and the coil needs a flyback diode. A MOSFET works with voltage and must be logic level. `analogWrite` sets the motor's speed with the PWM duty cycle.
