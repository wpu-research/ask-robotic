### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to combine a sensor, a decision rule and two outputs in one program
- How to set the fan speed from the temperature with `map`
- How to write an on/off decision with hysteresis
- How to manage a fan and a servo in the same loop without one blocking the other

### What you should know first
The serial monitor from lesson 9, working out temperature with an NTC from lesson 13, `map` and `constrain` from lesson 14, the servo from lesson 21, a fan with a transistor from lesson 22, hysteresis from lesson 24.

### The system has three parts
This project is made of input, decision and output.

```
Input:     NTC thermistor (A2)  ->  temperature
Decision:  >= 30 °C on, < 28 °C off (hysteresis)
Output:    fan speed (D6, PWM) + servo sweep (D9)
```

When you write a project, first separate these three parts, then test them one by one: first print the temperature to serial, then add the fan, and the servo last.

### Reading the temperature
Because the NTC is at the bottom and the 10 kΩ fixed resistor is at the top, you use `rntc = R * adc / (1023 - adc)`. As it warms up, the NTC's resistance falls: 10 kΩ at 25 °C, about 4.3 kΩ at 45 °C. So the voltage on A2 drops from 2.5 V to about 1.5 V. `constrain(adc, 1, 1022)` prevents division by zero.

### Linking speed to temperature
```
int hiz = map((int)(sicak * 10), 300, 500, 90, 255);
hiz = constrain(hiz, 90, 255);
```
Because `map` works with integers, we multiply the temperature by 10; the 30.0-50.0 °C range becomes 300-500. The output starts at 90, because a small motor does not turn at very low PWM. For 40 °C, the speed is 172.

### Decision: hysteresis
The fan switches on at 30 °C and above, but only switches off below 28 °C. At 29 °C the fan keeps its old state. For this, a `fanAcik` (fan on) variable is kept.

### Sharing without waiting
If the servo sweep were done in one go with `for`, the temperature would not be read until the sweep finished. Instead, each time `loop` runs, it moves the servo by only 2 degrees. With the same idea, the serial output is printed once every 25 rounds. Because the Servo library switches off PWM on D9 and D10, the fan is on D6.

### Summary
The project combines measuring with an NTC, a decision with hysteresis, and fan and servo outputs. The speed is converted to the 90-255 range with `map`, and the servo moves step by step, so the temperature stays up to date every round. Test the parts one by one first, then combine them.
