### One digit, one byte
A 7-segment display is seven small LED bars arranged side by side. The bars are named with the letters a, b, c, d, e, f, g; the eighth LED is known as the dot (dp). Showing a digit means choosing which bars light up. Because the eight LEDs have two states (on or off), the choice is exactly one **byte**: eight **bits**.

```
bit:     7   6   5   4   3   2   1   0
bar:    dp   g   f   e   d   c   b   a
```

Example: for the digit 1 only b and c light up. Bit 1 and bit 2 are 1: `0b00000110`. In hexadecimal notation this number is `0x06`. Hexadecimal notation shows a byte with two characters: `0x3F` = 3 × 16 + 15 = 63, and this number is the digit 0 (bars a-f are lit, g is off).

### The pin problem and its solution
Driving the display directly needs 8 pins. If there are other parts too, the Uno soon runs out of pins. The **74HC595** solves this: the Uno sends it the bits **one after another** over a single wire (SER), the chip lines them up in its internal register and drives eight outputs (QA-QH) at once. Three Uno pins are needed:

| Chip pin | Job | Pin used in the lesson |
|---|---|---|
| SER | Data: the bits arrive on this wire | D4 |
| SRCLK | Clock: each pulse shifts one bit in | D6 |
| RCLK | Latch: copies the shifted byte to the outputs | D5 |

### shiftOut and the latch
The command `shiftOut(VERI, SAAT, MSBFIRST, bayt)` sends eight bits one after another. `MSBFIRST` says the most significant bit (bit 7) goes first. After eight pulses, the first bit sent has travelled the farthest (QH), and the last bit sent, bit 0, has reached QA; that is why bit 0 lights the "a" bar.

While the bits are shifting in, the outputs do not change. The outputs are only updated together when the **latch** pin goes from LOW to HIGH. This way the display changes in one go instead of "sliding":

```
digitalWrite(MANDAL, LOW);
shiftOut(VERI, SAAT, MSBFIRST, desen);
digitalWrite(MANDAL, HIGH);
```

### Chaining
If you connect the first chip's serial output to the input of a second 74HC595, the two chips work one after the other. You still use only three Uno pins; `shiftOut` is called twice and you get 16 outputs. The cost is time: for every change of an output, all the bytes are sent again.

### Summary
A digit is an eight-bit byte whose bits match the bars. With the 74HC595 you send this byte one bit after another over three pins; `shiftOut` shifts the bits, and the outputs are updated when the latch goes HIGH. If you forget the latch, the display never changes. Every bar must have its own series resistor.
