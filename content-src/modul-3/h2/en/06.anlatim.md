### What you will learn in this lesson
- How to make a pin an input and read it with `digitalRead`
- The problem called a **floating input** and why it gives random readings
- How to read a button with only two wires (pin and GND) using `INPUT_PULLUP`
- How to make sense of the back-to-front "pressed = LOW" logic

### What you should know first
`setup()`, `loop()`, HIGH and LOW from lesson 1; Ohm's law (V = I × R) from lesson 2; `pinMode`, `digitalWrite` and closed circuits from lesson 3.

### The board is listening now
Until now the pins have always been **outputs**: the board wrote HIGH or LOW to the pin. For a button we want the opposite: the board should **read** the voltage coming from outside. For this the pin becomes an input, and `digitalRead(pin)` looks at the voltage on the pin and returns `HIGH` or `LOW`. In the lab model the threshold is 2.5 V: above it counts as HIGH, below it as LOW.

### Floating input
You connected one end of the button to D2 and the other end to GND. While the button is open, D2 is not connected to anything. A pin left unconnected like this is called a **floating input**. In a real circuit, electrical noise from the surroundings makes it show HIGH sometimes and LOW at other times; the reading is random. The lab also gives a warning for an unconnected input. The solution: "pull" the pin to a known voltage while it is open.

### Pull-up resistor
Inside every input pin of the Uno there is an optional resistor. `INPUT_PULLUP` switches it on and pulls the pin towards 5 V. In the lab model this resistor is 35 kΩ.

```
5 V ──[ internal pull-up, 35 kΩ ]──┬── D2 (digitalRead looks here)
                                   │
                               [ BUTTON ]
                                   │
                                  GND
```

State table:

| Button | Voltage on D2 | digitalRead(2) |
|---|---|---|
| Not pressed | 5 V (the internal resistor pulls it up) | HIGH |
| Pressed | about 0 V (connected to GND) | LOW |

While the button is pressed, a small current flows from 5 V to GND through the internal resistor: 5 V ÷ 35 000 Ω ≈ 0.14 mA. This is very small and puts no strain on anything. You used Ohm's law here as well.

### Back-to-front logic
With `INPUT_PULLUP`, **pressed = LOW**. It feels back to front at first, but it makes the circuit simpler because no outside resistor is needed. If you write the `digitalRead` result straight to an LED, the LED is on when you are not pressing. Because HIGH = 1 and LOW = 0, the operation `1 - durum` flips the value: 1 becomes 0 and 0 becomes 1. In the next lesson you will write this more readably with `if`.

In short, the reading pattern has three steps: `pinMode(pin, INPUT_PULLUP)` in `setup()`, `digitalRead(pin)` in `loop()`, then making sense of the result.

### Summary
Input pins read voltage, the opposite of output pins. A pin left unconnected floats and reads at random; `INPUT_PULLUP` prevents this by pulling the pin to 5 V through an internal resistor. Because the button connects the pin to GND, pressed reads LOW and not pressed reads HIGH.
