### Sound is a vibration
Sound is the air vibrating very fast. The number of vibrations in one second is called the **frequency**; its unit is the **hertz** (Hz). The higher the frequency, the higher the pitch. The time of one vibration, the **period**, is 1 / frequency. For 440 Hz, one round takes 1000 / 440, about 2.27 ms.

| Note | Frequency (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Do (C4) | 262 Hz |
| Mi (E4) | 330 Hz |
| Sol (G4) | 392 Hz |
| La (A4) | 440 Hz |
| Do (C5) | 523 Hz |

The same note one **octave** higher has about twice the frequency: the La above 440 Hz is 880 Hz.

### Active and passive buzzers
An **active buzzer** has its own oscillator inside; give it 5 V and it always beeps at the same pitch (about 2000 Hz in the lab). You cannot change the pitch. A **passive buzzer** is just a membrane; the pitch of the sound is set by the vibration you send. To play notes you need a passive buzzer. In the lab, the **Type** field in the properties panel decides this.

### tone() and noTone()
- `tone(pin, frekans)` produces a square wave at that frequency on the pin, and it lasts until you call `noTone(pin)`.
- `tone(pin, frekans, süre)` plays for only `süre` (duration) milliseconds and then stops by itself.

In a square wave the pin is HIGH for the same time as it is LOW. Because `tone` uses a timer in the background, it does **not block**: the command starts the sound and the program moves straight on to the next line. If you want to wait for the note to finish, you write a `delay` yourself. If you call `tone` several times in a row without waiting, each one replaces the previous one and only the last is heard.

```
tone(8, 262, 400);
delay(500);          // 400 ms sound + 100 ms silence
tone(8, 330, 400);
delay(500);
```

### Timer clash
On the Uno, `tone` uses the timer called Timer2. This timer also produces the PWM on D3 and D11. So while `tone` is playing, brightness control with `analogWrite` on D3 and D11 breaks; only fully on (255) or fully off (0) is safe. D9 and D10 are not affected. Connect LEDs that need brightness control to D9 or D10.

### Summary
Sound is vibration, and the frequency sets the pitch: when the frequency goes up the sound gets higher, and when it doubles it rises by one octave. To play notes you need a passive buzzer and `tone`. `tone` does not wait, so you put a `delay` between notes. While `tone` is playing, you cannot control brightness with PWM on D3 and D11; use D9 and D10 for dimmable LEDs.
