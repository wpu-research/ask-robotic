### Three LEDs in one case
An **RGB LED** is three tiny LEDs, red (R), green (G) and blue (B), placed inside the same case. It has four legs. In the **common cathode** type, the cathodes of the three LEDs join at one **COM** leg. COM is connected to GND. The R, G and B legs each go to a separate PWM pin.

```
D9  --[330 Ω]-- R \
D10 --[330 Ω]-- G  >-- LED --- COM --- GND
D11 --[330 Ω]-- B /
```

Each colour leg must have its own resistor. A single resistor would share the current of the three channels, and the colours would spoil each other's brightness.

### Colours add up in light
When three lights meet at the same spot, the colours add up:

| Red | Green | Blue | Colour you see |
|---|---|---|---|
| full | full | off | yellow |
| off | full | full | cyan |
| full | off | full | purple |
| full | full | full | white |
| off | off | off | black (LED off) |

Each channel's value is chosen from 0 to 255. Together, the three channels give 256 × 256 × 256, about 16.7 million different colours. If the three values are equal, you get shades of grey: (60, 60, 60) is dark grey, (200, 200, 200) is light grey. For orange, red is full and green is low: (255, 80, 0).

### Colour arrays and the Serial Monitor
To show many colours one after another, you use three arrays: `kirmizi[]`, `yesil[]`, `mavi[]` (red, green, blue). The three numbers at the same index describe one colour. You can type three numbers such as "255 128 0" into the Serial Monitor and read them with `parseInt`. If the user types 300, `constrain(değer, 0, 255)` keeps it at 255.

### Common anode and real brightness differences
On a **common anode** LED, COM is connected to 5 V, and a channel only lights while its pin is LOW. The logic flips: to make a channel as bright as `değer` (the value), you write `analogWrite(pin, 255 - değer)`. Which type you have is printed on the part, or shown in the properties panel in the lab.

In the lab model the forward voltages differ: red 1.9 V, green 2.2 V, blue 3.0 V. With 330 Ω the currents come out at about 8.6 mA, 7.8 mA and 5.6 mA. Blue is dimmer; that is why (255, 255, 255) may look slightly tinted rather than pure white. This is not a mistake.

### Summary
An RGB LED puts three LEDs in one case. With a common cathode, COM goes to GND and each channel is driven from its own PWM pin through its own resistor. Colours add up in light, and three values (R, G, B) describe millions of colours. On a common anode LED, COM goes to 5 V and the values are written the other way round: 255 - value.
