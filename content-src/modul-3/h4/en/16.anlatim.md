### Why is there no half brightness?
`digitalWrite` only knows two values: HIGH (5 V) and LOW (0 V). There is no command that "sets" the LED to 2.5 V. The solution is to switch the pin on and off very quickly. The eye does not see this fast flicker as separate flashes; it only sees the average. The same would happen if you switched a torch on and off hundreds of times a second: the light would look half as strong.

### PWM and duty cycle
**PWM** (pulse width modulation) means the pin is HIGH for part of each short, repeating round and LOW for the rest. The fraction of a round that is HIGH is called the **duty cycle**.

```
25% :  ▀▁▁▁▀▁▁▁▀▁▁▁   (1 of 4 parts HIGH)
50% :  ▀▀▁▁▀▀▁▁▀▀▁▁   (2 of 4 parts HIGH)
75% :  ▀▀▀▁▀▀▀▁▀▀▀▁   (3 of 4 parts HIGH)
```

On the Uno, the PWM frequency of D9, D10 and D11 is about 490 Hz. One round takes 1 / 490, about 2 ms. A 25% duty cycle means the pin is HIGH for about 0.5 ms of those 2 ms.

### analogWrite and the maths
In the `analogWrite(pin, değer)` command, the value `değer` is between 0 and 255:

- Duty cycle = `değer / 255`
- Average voltage is about `5 V × değer / 255`

Example: for `analogWrite(9, 102)` the duty cycle is 102 / 255 = 0.4, that is 40%, and the average voltage is 2 V. The ends are special: 0 makes the pin LOW all the time, 255 makes it HIGH all the time. No real wave is produced there.

### Which pins? And the potentiometer
Not every pin can do PWM. On the Uno the PWM pins are D3, D5, D6, D9, D10 and D11; on the board they have a `~` mark next to their numbers. On a pin without PWM, such as D13, `analogWrite` does not give half brightness; the pin only goes HIGH or LOW.

`analogRead` gives 0-1023, while `analogWrite` takes 0-255. To connect the two, you write `map(okunan, 0, 1023, 0, 255)`. Now, as you turn the potentiometer, the LED brightness changes.

### Summary
PWM produces an average voltage by switching the pin on and off very quickly. With `analogWrite(pin, değer)` you give a value from 0 to 255, and the duty cycle is value / 255. Only pins marked `~` can do PWM. By converting a 0-1023 reading, such as a potentiometer's, into 0-255 with `map`, you can set the brightness by hand.
