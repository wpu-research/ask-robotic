### NTC: the part whose resistance drops as it warms up
An **NTC** (Negative Temperature Coefficient) thermistor is a resistor whose resistance goes down as the temperature rises. The common type is 10 kΩ at 25 °C. The change is not linear but an exponential curve; the beta coefficient of the NTC in the lab is taken as B = 3950.

| Temperature | NTC resistance |
|---|---|
| 0 °C | ≈ 33.6 kΩ |
| 25 °C | 10 kΩ |
| 50 °C | ≈ 3.59 kΩ |
| 100 °C | ≈ 0.70 kΩ |

### The reading circuit
The top resistor is a fixed 10 kΩ, the bottom resistor is the NTC. When the temperature rises, the NTC gets smaller and the voltage on A2 drops. The NTC's resistance from the ADC value:

```
float r = 10000.0 * ham / (1024.0 - ham);
```

### The beta formula
With resistance R, reference R0 = 10 000 Ω (25 °C), and T0 = 298.15 K:

```
1/T = 1/T0 + log(R / R0) / B
```

T comes out in Kelvin; for Celsius you subtract 273.15. `log()` is the natural logarithm; it is not `log10()`. Check: if ham = 512, then R = 10 000 Ω, `log(1)` = 0, T = 298.15 K = 25 °C.

### Safety: an invalid reading
If `ham` is 0 or 1023, the division goes to zero or the result becomes meaningless. These extremes suggest that the sensor is disconnected or has a short circuit. Check for this case with an `if` before calculating.

### Summary
The NTC's resistance drops as it warms up, and not linearly. First the resistance is calculated from the raw value, then the temperature in Kelvin and Celsius with the beta formula. Do not forget to convert Kelvin to Celsius and to use the natural logarithm. A reading of 0 or 1023 is a sign of a sensor error.
